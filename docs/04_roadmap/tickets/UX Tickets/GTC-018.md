# GTC-018 — Host has no clear path forward after 5 of 5 complete

**Type:** UX
**Severity:** P4

---

### The Person

A host who has spent real time building this plan. They want to hand
over responsibility — not manage it indefinitely. They have just
finished assigning everything and are satisfied with the state of
the plan. They are ready to share it. Stage one feels done.

They arrived at this moment having completed every step Gather asked
of them: created the event, added details, added people, generated a
plan, run the plan check. They did everything right.

They are feeling: readiness. Quiet satisfaction. The anticipation
of release.

What they need to feel next: that Gather already knows what comes
next and is ready to carry it forward with them.

---

### The Broken Promise

Gather implicitly promised — through its five-step checklist, its
progress indicators, its plan check — that once the plan was ready,
guests would know what they needed to know. They would confirm or
decline. The host would see their decisions clearly and promptly.

What actually happens: the plan is never shared. Guests never receive
their invitations. They cannot confirm or decline because they never
see it. The checklist reaches 5 of 5 complete and goes silent.

What the user says: "I didn't know what to do next."

What Gather is failing to say — and already knows: everything is
ready. Here is how to move forward.

---

### The Cost

The host carries everything back. Every responsibility they thought
Gather had taken from them returns. They will have to follow up
themselves — tell everyone what they need to bring, how much, when.
Gather becomes a hurdle in the organisation process rather than a
tool that shares the load.

The user leaves and does not return. They may tell others the app
isn't useful. This is not a minor UX gap — it is the gate between
effort and payoff. Every prior step was preparation for this moment.
If this moment fails, all of it fails with it.

---

### The Fix

A button — front and centre, immediately visible at 5 of 5 complete
— that speaks in Gather's voice and takes on the next action.

What the user feels when it works: lightness. Something lifted. The
sense that the product already knew they were coming and was ready
for them.

What it looks like: a prominent primary button, positioned directly
below or adjacent to the completed checklist, reading:

"Move to confirming and I'll let everyone know what they need to do"

Gather speaks. The host releases.

What makes it feel like Gather: the button uses first person — Gather
takes on the voice of the coordinator. It doesn't say "Next step" or
"Continue." It says what Gather will do. That is the product's
character.

---

### Acceptance

Felt: A first-time host reaching 5 of 5 complete immediately sees
the next move. They do not need to scroll, search, or ask what comes
next. The product is already there.

Functional: Button visible without scrolling on the plan dashboard
at 5 of 5 complete. Clicking it initiates the Gate Check transition
to CONFIRMING and triggers participant notifications.

Must never: Replace or duplicate the Gate Check card. The button
invokes the Gate Check flow — it does not bypass it. If Gate Check
finds blockers, they surface before transition. If Gate Check passes,
transition proceeds. The Gate Check card remains on the dashboard.
