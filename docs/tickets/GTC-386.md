### Scope (Nigel's words quoted; Cowork's findings after each)

**Item 66 - "Divide 'The message shown' into its parts".** Step 4 of the pre-flight (page.tsx about
L748, title `PREFLIGHT_STEP_TITLES[3]` "The message, shown", blurb "Exactly what each person will
receive, and who I'll nudge.") draws `MessageStep` (from about L1001) as one long run: the preview,
then the nudge sentence and its two choices (about L1521), then the host's list (about L1551).
RULED: "Three labelled parts (Recommended)". The three parts and their labels are, in this order:

1. "What guests will receive" - the preview.
2. "How I nudge" - the question and its choices. The sentence becomes Nigel's question: "When
   someone hasn't answered and has no mobile I can text, how do you want to handle it?" with the
   two existing choices kept as they are (`CHASE_DEFAULT_PILLS`: "Nudge them by email", "Leave them
   to me").
3. "Gather will not message these people" - the list.

Words live in `src/lib/preflight/ask-preview-compose.ts` (`CHASE_DEFAULT_SENTENCE` L282,
`HOST_LIST_HEADING` L246 "Yours to handle"). Pinned by tests not yet found: find every test that
pins the step's words, its order or the sentence, and say how each changes.

Things to check, not take from Cowork:

- a. The sentence today is a STATEMENT that changes with the choice ("...I'll nudge them by email."
  or "...I'll leave them to you."). His new words are a QUESTION. Do the choices still show which
  one is active, and does a host who has never chosen still see the default marked
  (`DEFAULT_SUFFIX`)? Say how the question and the active choice read together.

  asked" (group A, Gather will not message them) and "I'll ask, but won't nudge" (group B,

  may be false of anyone listed under it. Group B is messaged once. Say exactly what is true, then
  give Nigel options for where group B sits (for example under part 2 "How I nudge", where it is
  true, with only group A under part 3). Do not choose; put it as a question.
- c. The amber box above the preview (about L1315, "N people get no message") also names people
  Gather will not message. Say how it relates to part 3 and whether the two now say the same thing
  twice.
- d. Item 67 (a later ticket, GTC-387) renames "Yours to handle" and adds click-to-update. His
  ruling for 66 already gives part 3 the label "Gather will not message these people". Say plainly
  what of 67 this ticket takes (the label) and what it leaves (the reasons, click on a name to
  update an email or mobile, the two fields).
- e. Step 4's own blurb and the screen-reader or tick label: do they still make sense over three
  parts?
- f. The labels as headings: use an existing style, do not invent one. Look at the step on screen
  at 1280 and 390 first and say what the parts look like today.

**Item 68 - "Keep the ticks".** The five "Settled" ticks on the pre-flight are CLIENT STATE ONLY
(page.tsx about L209, `useState({})`), so a reload or leaving the page loses them. RULED: "In this
browser (Recommended)": remembered on the device, per event, no migration. His other choices (pace,
who gets nudged, the message) already save as he makes them; only the five ticks are lost. Plan:

- where it is stored (browser storage, key per event), reading it on load without a flash of
  unticked, and the page still working if storage is blocked or throws (private window, cleared
  data);
- how it meets `settled[n]` (steps that auto-count as settled; see about L287 and L389) so a
  remembered tick never hides something that is no longer settled: say whether a stored tick should
  be dropped when the facts behind it change, and propose the rule;
- what happens after the press (the event has been sent) and for a different host on the same
  browser;
- the existing test that pins "client state only" (GTC-189) and how it changes on purpose;
- tell Nigel plainly what "in this browser" means: a different phone or laptop starts unticked.

**How the words are written:** in the app, apostrophes in these words are curly, as the existing
lines are. These are screen words, not text-message words.
