## ✅ SCOPED 2026-09-28 — TWO FOUNDER RULINGS

### Ruling 1 — the text reminders say what the email reminders say, and name the event

Asked as: A) same words as the emails, opt-out line on its own line; **B) same words, but texts
say the event's name instead of "this one"**; C) something else. **The founder chose B.**

- The words are [[GTC-189]] slice 8b's ruled email reminder — `composeChase` in
  `src/lib/messages/chase-register.ts` (W6, W7, W8 and the 8b-hold rule) — with two changes, for
  text only:
  - **First reminder opener:** *"Hi {first} - Gather here again, helping {host} with {event}."*
    (the email's *"with this one"* becomes the event's name).
  - **Second reminder opener:** *"Hi {first} - Gather here, checking in once more for {host} about
    {event}."* The email's second opener has no *"this one"*, so the name needed a home. Proposed
    at scoping; the founder did not change it.
- Everything after the opener is the email's, unchanged: the not-heard sentence under the 8b-hold
  rule (*"you"* only when nothing at all is answered; otherwise the open things by name, carried
  children included) and the tap line.
- **The ground:** a text has no subject line, and every Gather text comes from one number, so a
  guest's texts for different events sit in one thread. The email's *"this one"* works because its
  subject names the event (`askSubject`).
- **This departs from [[GTC-187]] decision 5** (*"the body is identical"*) for the reminders only,
  knowingly. The ask keeps decision 5.
- It replaces `getFirstNudgeMessage` and `getSecondNudgeMessage` in
  `src/lib/sms/nudge-templates.ts` on the text chase (`drainChaseRow` in
  `src/lib/press/dispatch.ts`). They say *"{host} is waiting for your response for {event}"* and
  *"Reminder: {host} needs your response for {event}. Please confirm"* to anyone with something
  open, whatever they have already answered; the second also breaks the register's *no "please"*
  rule (slice 2, answer 5).

### Ruling 2 — every text Gather sends ends with the opt-out line, on its own line

Asked as: **A) every text ends with "Reply STOP to opt out" on its own line — the invitation, the
thank-you, your own nudges and the "maybe" follow-up**; B) all of Gather's own texts but not the
host's own nudges; C) leave the other three as they are. **The founder chose A.**

- **The line:** `Reply STOP to opt out`, after a line break, with no dash. Exactly once per text.
- **It reaches every text:**
  - the invitation by text — `drainOnce` in `src/lib/press/dispatch.ts`, which also carries
    mini-sends and the resend door's text sends. **None today**, although [[GTC-189]] slice 5's
    plan said the dispatcher would send the ask *"with the STOP suffix"*;
  - both text reminders (ruling 1);
  - the decide-by follow-up — `getDecideByFollowupMessage` in `src/lib/sms/nudge-templates.ts`,
    whose ` — Reply STOP to opt out` becomes the line;
  - the wrap-up thank-you — `buildSmsWrapUpMessage` in `src/lib/sms/wrap-up-templates.ts`.
    **None today**;
  - a host's by-hand nudge sent as a text — the route at
    `src/app/api/events/[id]/people/[personId]/nudge/route.ts`. **None today.**
- **The ground:** the invitation is the first text a phone-only guest ever gets, from a number they
  do not know. A STOP reply is already honoured on every path (Zone 7); the line tells people they
  can. The code already states the rule — `getDecideByFollowupMessage`'s comment calls the suffix
  *"mandatory on every system-sent template"* — and three of the five texts break it.
- **The law, recorded and not relied on:** the Unsolicited Electronic Messages Act 2007, s11,
  requires a functional unsubscribe facility in *commercial* electronic messages. Whether Gather's
  texts are commercial is arguable. The ruling does not rest on it.
