### Founder rulings — second pass (Nigel, 2026-09-13)

Made the same day, on the audit below. Each is recorded with the founder's words where the
founder gave them.

#### A. Children in the host's OWN household are hers — CORRECTED, see below

The audit found that a child in the host's household has the host as contact ([[GTC-256]]
Ruling 7), the host is never messaged (Ruling 5), and so the child's ask reaches nobody — a hole
in the one household every event has.

**As first ruled, 2026-09-13:**

> A child in the host's household is hers to handle. Their item appears on her list, the same
> list as an unreachable adult, rather than being carried in a message she will never receive.
> The list is now two things: people Gather cannot reach, and children whose contact is her.
> She is not chased about her own children.

⚠ **CORRECTED THE SAME DAY, 2026-09-13 — the founder narrowed it, and the correction is the
ruling. This is not the original.** The phrase *"children whose contact is her"* was wider than
intended: it would have swept in a child of another household that has picked the host as its
contact.

> A child in the HOST'S OWN HOUSEHOLD is hers to handle. A child in another household that has
> picked her as its contact is not — GTC-256 already ruled she receives those as that household's
> ear, and my wording would have moved them onto her list for no reason.

**So the ruling is:**
- A child **in the host's own household**: their item is a line on her list, beside the
  unreachable adults. She is not chased about her own children.
- A child in **any other** household whose picked contact is the host: carried to her in a
  message, exactly as [[GTC-256]] founder answer 2 ruled — *"She volunteered to be their ear"* —
  and that household's own switch governs it.

**Consequences recorded:**
- The host's list holds two kinds of line, each named with its item: adults Gather cannot reach,
  and children of her own household.
- **The host can therefore receive an ask message — as a carrier, and only as a carrier.** That
  consequence has its own heading, A2, directly below.

#### A2. ⚠ The host as carrier — the one exception to the host exclusion

*Given its own heading on the founder's instruction, 2026-09-13: it is the one place ruling A as
corrected reaches into guarded behaviour, and slice 1 must meet it rather than discover it.*

**The exception, stated as narrowly as it is.** The host exclusion holds everywhere except in one
case: **the host is another household's picked contact, and that household has a child who holds
an item.** Then that child's ask is carried to her, in a message that names the child's item and
nothing of hers.

**What does not move:**
- **She is never sent her own ask.** No message to her names an item she holds, and no message is
  sent to her on her own account.
- **[[GTC-256]] Ruling 5 is untouched. She is not a message recipient in her own right.**
  `isAddressable` and `ADDRESSABLE_PERSON_EVENT` in `src/lib/eligibility/host-exclusion.ts` go on
  excluding her from every path they guard, and `HOST_SKIP_REASON` — *"never receives her own
  ask"* — stays true as written.
- **[[GTC-256]] founder answer 2 is what makes the exception legitimate rather than a breach:**
  *"She volunteered to be their ear."* She is reached because another household chose her, not
  because of what she is.

**Narrow by construction, not only by rule.** [[GTC-256]] Ruling 7 makes her the
`PRIMARY_CONTACT` of her own household and bars her joining another as a member, and
`resolveHouseholdChannel` falls back only to a household's own primary contact. So she is another
household's contact **only by an explicit pick** of `Household.contactPersonEventId`, never by
default. The children of her own household are not carried to her at all — they are on her list
(ruling A).

**⚠ THIS IS THE ONLY CASE IN THE MODEL WHERE THE HOST RECEIVES AN ASK.** Anyone widening it later
— to her own items, to the adults of a household she is contact for, to a household that did not
pick her — is **widening a ruling, not fixing a gap**, and needs a founder ruling to do it.

**What slice 1 meets:**
- The chooser admits her **only** when all three facts hold — another household, she is its picked
  contact, a child of that household holds an item — and that household's `resolveHouseholdMuted`
  reads as sending.
- A message to her carries carried items only. Nothing of hers is ever merged into it.
- A test pins both directions: the carried ask to her is produced, her own ask is not, and
  `test:host-never-messaged` stays green unchanged.

**⚠ Where it reaches a Do-Not-Touch zone — Zone 3, and it is not solved here.** The host
exclusion itself is [[GTC-256]] Ruling 5's invariant rather than one of the eight named zones. The
zone this exception reaches is **Zone 3**: a carried message needs a link she can answer from, and
Ruling 8 gives her **no PARTICIPANT token and no guest-side page** — `ensureEventTokens` in
`src/lib/tokens.ts` revokes any PARTICIPANT token held by a `role: 'HOST'` row (the
`hostRowPersonIds` sweep, [[GTC-256]] phase 3, approved narrow). How the host answers a carried
child's item is therefore **open** — unanswered decision 14 — and is not to be settled in passing
by issuing her a token. *[Answered 2026-09-14 by ruling N — a one-off link that answers only the
carried item, the single exception to [[GTC-256]] Ruling 8. Filed as [[GTC-297]]; Zone 3 is still
not solved here.]*

#### C. `contactMethod` — the defect is that it is wrong, and it is wrong today

**The finding is not the derivation. It is that 184 of 269 values are wrong** — in gather_dev,
of 269 adult non-host memberships (as of 2026-09-13), 184 carry a `contactMethod` their own
`Person` fields contradict. **This is a defect that exists now, and it went unnoticed because one
path reads the column.** That path is `findProxyNudgeCandidates`, which skips a household channel
whose stored value is not `SMS` — so a contact with a valid phone stored as `NONE` or `EMAIL` is
never reached by it, and nothing else would notice.

How the tree lets it drift (verified by reading, as of `b3e9f31`):

- **A per-event snapshot of a global record.** `Person` is shared across events; `contactMethod`
  lives on each `PersonEvent` and is recomputed only on the event being edited
  (`reconcileHouseholdMembers`, `PATCH` in `src/app/api/p/[token]/contact-info/route.ts`).
- **An edit that never recomputes.** `PATCH` in `src/app/api/events/[id]/people/[personId]/route.ts`
  writes `email` and `phoneNumber` and leaves `contactMethod` alone.
- **Two phone columns.** `POST` in the batch-import route stores the legacy `Person.phone`, and it
  and `createMember` count that as a phone; every sender reads `phoneNumber`. Such a row says `SMS`
  and can never be texted.
- **Fixtures write it directly.**

All 184 rows sit on fixture and test events, so they measure drift in this database, not in real
capture; which mechanism produced each row was not established.

**The two pure choosers replace it** (slice 1): the ask's and the chase's, reading `email`,
`phoneNumber` and `smsOptedOut` at the moment of sending.

**Then the column is dropped, not kept** — *"a column five places write and nothing reads is the
drift we just measured."* The sequence: slice 8 stops the last read; then stop writing and keep
it for one ticket; then drop it and the `ContactMethod` enum in its own migration. **Filed as
[[GTC-295]]**, on the founder's instruction, rather than left outside the slices and unowned.

`reachabilityTier` is written at the same five sites from the same fields and has the same shape;
it is read for display by `invite-status` and `ReachabilityBar`. It is named and excluded in
[[GTC-295]], and is not covered by this finding or the ruling.

#### ⚠ THE DEFAULT — measured against gather_dev, and I think it is wrong

ruling is a workaround for a bad default. Measured read-only on 2026-09-15, reusing `chooseAskRoute`

**⚠ A CORRECTION INSIDE THIS MEASUREMENT, RECORDED BECAUSE IT REVERSED THE CONCLUSION.** The first
pass counted every `chooseChaseRoute` answer of kind `DIRECT` as *chased by text* without reading
its `channel`. Because the narrow-reading line is still in the tree, most of those answers were
`EMAIL`. That pass reported a tick population of 7.9% — small enough to call the default fine. Split
by channel, the same query reports **95.1%**. The first number was wrong and the conclusion built on

**The numbers, as they stand:**

- **253 of 266 recipients would be tickable — 95.1%.** Only **7 (2.6%)** have a usable phone and are

- **Across every `Person` row in gather_dev: 333 people, and 12 have a `phoneNumber` stored at all.

1. **Capture asks for neither.** In `Moment1InputForm`, both Email and Phone are rendered as

**Not decided here, and not treated as decided.** AG is recorded as ruled; this section is the

### ⚠ Finding — TWO checks caught today only because their results were checked rather than believed

*Recorded together, because they are one defect in two places and the pairing is the point. Neither
was found by being careful; both were found because an answer was read instead of accepted.*

**1. M2's substitution did not land, and the run after it read exactly GREEN.**

`return { success: false, error: message };` occurs **three times** in `src/lib/email.ts` — once in
`resultOf` and once in each sender's `catch`. The substitution asserted on a unique match, aborted,
and left the file unmutated. The suite then ran and reported **25/27: byte-identical to GREEN.**

**Read as a mutation result, that says the assertion is weak and the placeholder is unpinned. It says
nothing of the kind. The mutation was never there.** Retargeted on the surrounding context, verified
present by grep at line 89, and it then failed the one assertion it should and nothing else.

**⚠ THIS IS [[GTC-277]] FINDING 8'S RULE BITING IN THE FILE THAT RECORDS IT.** That finding is
titled *"a mutation that reports nothing has TWO explanations"*, and
`tests/email-send-result-test.ts` already carries a twenty-line comment about the same thing
happening to GTC-265's own mutation M3, ending: *"a mutation that reports no failures has made two
claims, not one — that the assertion is weak, AND that the mutation applied. Verify the second before
believing the first."* **It happened again, in that file, to the next person to run a mutation
against it.** The comment is not enough; only the aborting assertion caught it.

⚠ **A citation to correct, and not here.** That comment attributes the rule to *"GTC-267's"*.
[[GTC-267]] is the unauthenticated-reads ticket, whose evidence merely lists mutation results;
**the rule is [[GTC-277]] Finding 8's.** Left as written because it is [[GTC-265]]'s prose and not
slice 4b's to rewrite — named so whoever owns it can fix it, per the Citations rule.

**2. The NUL-byte check, at slice 4a, earlier the same day.**

Written as `grep -qU $'\x00'` under the Bash tool's zsh. A shell cannot pass a NUL in an argument, so
**the pattern was EMPTY and matched every file** — it reported a NUL byte in all six changed files,
generated migration SQL included. Re-run in Python against the raw bytes, all six are clean.

**What the two have in common, and the rule they leave.** Both are checks whose *result* was
implausible enough to be looked at — six files out of six including one Prisma had just written; a
mutation run identical to GREEN to the assertion. **A quieter version of either would have passed
and been believed.** The NUL grep hitting one file, or the mutation shifting one unrelated assertion,
and neither would have been questioned. So the rule is not *be careful writing checks*; it is:

> **A check's output is evidence about the check as well as about the code. Read it as both. And a
> claim that a check can only make if its own instrument worked — a mutation's absence of failures,
> a grep's absence of matches — is a claim about the instrument first.**

It is the same family as [[GTC-273]]'s detector matching the spelling of a credential rather than
evaluating it, [[GTC-276]]'s detector inheriting the defect the ticket that wrote it had just named,
ruling AN's two refusals sharing a why-code, and ruling AH's measurement reading an answer's kind and
never its channel. **Five instances now sit in this ledger and two of them are from today.**

### ⚠ The honest limit — what this change cannot prove, and what closes it

**What is proven:** the read of Resend's documented success envelope. The SDK's own type is
`CreateEmailResponseSuccess { id: string }`, and the suite's success stub already models it — it
