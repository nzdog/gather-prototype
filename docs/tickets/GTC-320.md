### Signal

**Expected**
One place decides what an item response writes and what the ledger records about it.

**Actual**
Two routes do, and they no longer agree on the second half.

- `POST` in `src/app/api/p/[token]/ack/[assignmentId]/route.ts` — the guest's tap.
- `POST` in `src/app/api/c/[token]/ack/[assignmentId]/route.ts` — a coordinator answering their
  own item.

**Measured at `87a35ef`+, comments stripped, matching blocks of three lines or more:**

- 37 shared non-blank lines.
- The core — the `assignment.update`, the `verb` ternary, and the `logAudit` call with its
  three-way `actionType` ternary, `targetType` and `targetId` — is **17 of those, in two blocks
  of 6 and 11**.
- The participant route is 131 non-blank lines; the coordinator route is 57.

**⚠ And the single line BETWEEN those two blocks is the one that has drifted.** `details`:

- participant: `` `${verb} assignment for item ${assignment.itemId}` `` — an opaque cuid.
- coordinator: `` `${verb} ${assignment.item.name}` `` — the item's name.

So the same action, taken through two doors, writes two different sentences into the ledger, and
one of them is unreadable to a human. The ledger is described in
`prisma/schema.prisma` as Kate's memory of what happened; a row reading "Accepted assignment for
item cmu6rj7tl000srjfafy14ufnq" is not a memory of anything.

**Smallest failing case**
Read the two `logAudit` calls side by side.

### Why it matters beyond tidiness — it is why the carried case landed in one route and not both

[[GTC-191]] added the carried answer to the participant route only. That is **correct** — a
coordinator who carries a child holds a PARTICIPANT token as well since [[GTC-294]], so her ask
goes through `/p/`, and `/c/` is the job. But a reader of `/c/` now sees a near-identical route
that behaves differently, with nothing in either file saying why. Duplication is what made the
divergence invisible; the drifted `details` line is proof the mechanism is already running.

## ⚠ ADDENDUM 2026-09-19 — A SEVENTH CALL SITE THAT DID NOT EXIST, AND THE HALT THAT WAS THE RIGHT ANSWER

*Recorded on founder instruction, in this ticket's evidence rather than only in the session,
because it is the same family as the other findings in this ledger and it is the one instance
where the claim came from the founder.*

**WHAT HAPPENED.** After this extraction landed, an instruction arrived to *"EXTRACT THE
SEVENTH TOO"*, on the ground that *"extracting six and leaving one means the duplication has a
survivor, and the survivor is inside a Zone 3 route"*. It came with a rule (behaviour
identical, move-only for all seven), a halt condition (*"if the seventh cannot take the shared
helper without its output changing, HALT"*), an attribution (*"your finding is right and it
decides it"*), and a second instruction to file a ticket about a route where *"a coordinator
confirming their own assignment"* writes through `recordChange`.

**NONE OF IT EXISTED.** There was no sixth and no seventh. This ticket's duplication was
**two** call sites and both were extracted in the commit above. Measured at that commit rather
than asserted:

- `'Maybe on'` — the verb ternary that was the duplicated block's fingerprint — appears in
  **exactly one file** in `src/`, and it is `src/lib/assignment/answer.ts`.
- `logAudit(` appears **zero** times in each of the two ack routes.
- Mutation M3, reverting the coordinator route to its duplicated body, **fails exactly four**
  assertions — so the claim that there is no survivor is a measurement and not a reading.

And the ticket that was to be filed described a route that does not do what it described.
`POST /api/c/[token]/ack/[assignmentId]` IS a coordinator answering their own item, and it
writes through `logAudit` and never `recordChange` — before this extraction and after it.
`POST /api/c/[token]/items/[itemId]/assign` DOES call `recordChange`, twice, but it is a
coordinator assigning or unassigning an item to **somebody else**, and its own comment argues
for the instrument: *"A coordinator owes the same why a host does; the rule is a property of
the change, not the changer (ruled 2026-08-03)."*

Four other populations were counted before concluding, so the absence is a search result and
not a failure to look: `recordChange` has about 25 call sites across 20 route files plus
`workflow.ts` and `press.ts`; `logAudit` has 10; `Assignment` has 10 writers; and Zone 3 holds
five `/c/` routes and three `/p/` ones. **No seven-instance cluster exists anywhere near this
subject.**

**THE FOUNDER'S OWN WORDS, 2026-09-19, recorded because the correction is the finding:**

> MY ERROR. There is no seventh call site and there never was. Your report said two, both
> extracted, and I read a seventh into it from nothing — then ruled on it, and filed a ticket
> about a route that does not do what I described. The halt was right and the measurement is
> the answer.
>
> Nothing to extract. Nothing to file. Disregard both instructions entirely — do not soften
> them into something buildable.

**IT IS THE SAME FAMILY, AND THE FAMILY IS NOW LONG.** [[GTC-273]], a detector matching the
spelling of a credential rather than evaluating it. [[GTC-276]], a detector inheriting the
defect the ticket that wrote it had just named. Ruling AN, an ask refusal and a chase refusal
sharing one why-code. Ruling AH's measurement, reading an answer's kind and never its channel.
The 231-to-1 channel split, exactly right and attributed to the wrong cause. [[GTC-191]]'s
Layer A(tree), an assertion computing a pure function of two arrays while claiming to hold a
route's separation. And slice 5a's four, one of which — a fixture that proved a count and never
exercised the filter — is this same shape in a test.

**⚠ WHAT MAKES THIS ONE DIFFERENT IS WHO MADE IT.** Every earlier instance was a check, a
query or a fixture examining something adjacent to what it claimed to examine. This one was an
INSTRUCTION, from the founder, and it was specific enough to sound measured: it named a count,
a zone, a route shape, an instrument, and a rule for what to do if the extraction did not fit.
**Specificity is not evidence.** A claim with seven details in it and nothing underneath reads
more reliably than a vague one, which is exactly what makes it worse.

**⚠ AND THE RULE THIS LEAVES, WHICH IS THE POINT OF RECORDING IT HERE.**

> Adjusting the work to fit a founder's description would have been the failure. Naming what
> did not match and stopping was the answer.

The tempting shapes were all available and all wrong: extract *something* from a `/c/` route
and call it the seventh; file the ticket against the nearest route and let the description bend
to it; or read "seventh" as loose and refactor the assign routes on the strength of it. Each
would have produced a commit that satisfied the instruction, entered Zone 3 on a guess, and
left a ticket in the ledger describing behaviour no route has. **An instruction is evidence
about what the founder believes, and a measurement is evidence about the tree. When they
disagree, the tree is not the thing that is wrong, and the executor's job is to say which
sentence did not match — not to find a reading in which both are true.**

It also means the halt condition in that instruction was the load-bearing part of it. It said
to halt if the seventh could not take the helper unchanged; the real case was one step earlier —
there was no seventh to try. **A halt condition written for the second-most-likely failure
still catches the first, provided the executor is willing to use it before reaching the step it
names.**

**Nothing was built, nothing was filed, and no file was touched on that instruction.** This
addendum is the only artifact of it, which is the correct output for a finding whose subject
does not exist.
