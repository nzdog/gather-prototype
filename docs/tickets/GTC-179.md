# GTC-179 — E2: Cadence controls — pre-flight pace + per-person go-gentle — CLOSED

**Status:** Complete | **Branch:** `feat/moment-one-redesign` |

**Effort:** high (filed `e5f6e82`; executed 2026-08-27 in four committable phases — the planned

⚠ **CLOSED AS MACHINERY. NEITHER CONTROL IS REACHABLE BY A HOST.** Both settings are stored and
obeyed end to end, on the direct and proxy send paths; **no surface sets either one** until
[[GTC-188]] (I1) builds the pre-flight. Stated here, at the top, rather than left for the

*Effort level is a proposal from the discovery-report ticket breakdown — run `/effort` manually

### Context

Moment 4 spec §10.3: cadence controls live where the people-decisions live. **Per-event pace**: at
the pre-flight (*nudge pace: standard / relaxed / off*) — a sending decision made where Kate
reviews how everyone is contacted. **Per-person**: a mark alongside the recipient picker (*go
gentle / don't chase*), set at Moment 1 or any time — a hosting judgement the system stores and
obeys. **Don't-chase is the far end** — the per-person off-switch (the mother, the bereaved, the
feuding cousin). Gentle means fewer/softer. **Not a settings page.**

Today: no cadence config field anywhere. The only "gentle" concept is `HostNudgeVariant`
(`warm|casual|gentle|direct`), a message *tone* choice for one-off manual nudges via
`NudgeComposer.tsx` — unrelated to adjusting automated cadence.

### Founder Rulings (2026-08-27) — Phase 0 gate

*Four rulings closing the Phase 0 gate raised by the audit pass. Every finding the audit
reported was checked against the tree at `aa64a5f`, not against ticket text. Rulings 1-4
unblock execution; the audit items recorded after them are carried into scope, and the five

**Ruling 1 — GENTLE means one nudge at day 5. Offsets `[5]`. After that the system stops.**
Moment 4 §10.3 defines gentle only as "fewer/softer" — no day count exists anywhere in the
spec, the Hinge spec, or the discovery report (grep-verified across `docs/03_specs`,
`docs/04_roadmap` and `docs/tickets`). This supplies the number.

**Gentle does not hand back to the host any differently from anyone else.** Whatever handback
exists once [[GTC-251]] (E6) lands applies to gentle exactly as it applies to a spent standard
cadence. **Gentle is a volume control, not a handback control** — it changes how many messages
a person receives, and nothing about what the system does when it runs out of them. Recorded
explicitly because the opposite reading is available and wrong: "the system stops after day 5"
describes the cadence ending, not a different ending.

**Ruling 2 — RELAXED means two nudges at days 6 and 12. Offsets `[6, 12]`.** Same absence in
the spec, same remedy. Two legs, as standard has, spaced wider.

**Ruling 3 — don't-chase suppresses the proxy path. A person marked `DONT_CHASE` must receive
no automated message on EITHER path.**

**Implement as SUPPRESSION ONLY** — a boolean read in `findProxyNudgeCandidates`
(`src/lib/sms/proxy-nudge-eligibility.ts`), gated **after** the child rule (`isMessageableRole`)
and **after** opt-out (`isOptedOut`), never through either. **Do NOT retime the proxy path.**
Retiming needs a decision about which clock a household nudge counts from, which is
[[GTC-252]]'s and is undecided; suppression needs no clock at all, which is exactly why the two
separate cleanly here.

This is **the paradigm case from §10.3**. The spec names "the mother" as the archetype for
don't-chase, and the mother is the person most likely to be her household's picked channel
(`Household.contactPersonEventId`, C1 / [[GTC-172]]). Without this ruling the direct path goes
silent while `findProxyNudgeCandidates` keeps messaging her on every cron tick — **the control
would fail precisely where it matters most.** Audit finding, ruled in.

Note what this does NOT do: it does not fix [[GTC-252]]'s unbounded re-nudge, which affects
every household regardless of any mark. That ticket is untouched and still open.

**Ruling 4 — composition of the two axes: QUIETER WINS.**

**The strict override ladder is wrong and must be corrected.** `resolveNudgeOffsetDays` in
`src/lib/nudge-cadence.ts` documents its intended precedence as "per-PERSON override, else
per-EVENT default, else the system default". Under that ladder a person marked `GENTLE` on an
`OFF` event resolves to `[5]` — **more nudges than an unmarked person on the same event, who
gets none.** A control whose only purpose is to reduce contact would increase it. The ladder's
docstring is corrected as part of Phase 1.

**The rule:** resolve each layer to a candidate offset array, then reduce by comparing them and
taking the quieter. **Quieter means FEWER NUDGES FIRST, THEN LATER** — count dominates; timing
breaks ties.

- `GENTLE [5]` on a `RELAXED [6, 12]` event resolves to **`[5]`**. One message beats two, even
  though it lands earlier. Stated by the founder as the worked example.
- `OFF` or `DONT_CHASE` resolves to `[]` and beats everything — zero is the fewest.
- A layer that is unset has **no opinion** and contributes no candidate. It is not the system
  default wearing a disguise; with both layers unset the default `[4, 7]` applies, which is what
  keeps `resolveNudgeOffsetDays({})` and `({ person: null, event: null })` at `[4, 7]`.

**The full composition matrix.** Rows are the per-person mark, columns the per-event pace.

| | pace unset | `STANDARD` `[4,7]` | `RELAXED` `[6,12]` | `OFF` `[]` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **mark unset** | `[4,7]` *(system default)* | `[4,7]` | `[6,12]` | `[]` |
| **`GENTLE`** `[5]` | `[5]` | `[5]` *(1 < 2)* | `[5]` *(1 < 2)* | `[]` *(0 < 1)* |
| **`DONT_CHASE`** `[]` | `[]` | `[]` | `[]` | `[]` |

The equal-length tie-break ("then later") is **unreachable with today's vocabulary** — no two
distinct values in the matrix share a length above zero, and a single-opinion cell has nothing
to compare against. It is implemented and tested directly against the comparator anyway, because
it is the ruled rule and the next pace value added would reach it silently.

**Ruling 5 — decision 10: DEDICATED TYPED COLUMNS, not `EventSetup` JSONB (2026-08-27).**
Settles the one audit decision left ambiguous at the Phase 0 gate. Rationale, recorded:

- **The nudge scheduler reads the pace on every 15-minute cron tick.** This is hot
  scheduler input, not form state.
- **`Event.decideByOffsetHours` is the existing precedent** for a scheduler-read tuning value,
  and it is a typed column. Ruling 4's resolver already mirrors that field's params-object and
  NULL-means-not-set conventions; matching its storage keeps one pattern rather than two.
- **`EventSetup`'s JSON tenants are Moment-2 form state** — `mainsData`, `dietaryData`,
  `extendedCategoriesData` and the rest. An unrelated concern; a cadence knob does not belong
  among them.
- **A typed enum gets database-level validity checking that JSONB does not.** An invalid pace
  is rejected by Postgres rather than discovered by a reader.
- **`PersonEvent` carries no JSON column**, so the mark had no alternative regardless. The
  event side is the only half where the question was live at all.

Consequence: two nullable enum columns and two enums, generated in phase 2 and surfaced for
sign-off before `prisma migrate dev` runs (Stop Condition 7).

The audit flagged that the resolver returns `[]` identically for don't-chase, for `OFF`, and for

**Ruling 7 — decision 9: mid-flight setting changes (2026-08-27).** Two parts.

**(a) What has been sent stays sent.** An ordinal stamp is never cleared or re-opened by a pace
change, **even if the new cadence would not have made that leg due yet.** A person stamped on day
4 under `STANDARD` whose host then switches to `RELAXED` `[6, 12]` keeps the stamp; the system
does not un-send, and does not re-send at day 6.

**(b) At most ONE nudge per person per run.** If a setting change makes several legs
simultaneously due — `OFF` to `STANDARD` on day 9, say — **send the earliest due leg only and let
the next tick handle the rest.**

**(b) is recorded as a general rule about the sweep, not a special case for pace changes.** It
binds every path into the sweep, whatever made the legs coincide.

⚠ **(b) IS A BUG FIX, NOT A REGRESSION GUARD — the sweep does NOT already behave this way, and it
is reachable today with no pace change involved.** `findNudgeCandidates` pushes to `eligibleFirst`
and `eligibleSecond` from two independent `if` statements with no `else` and no cross-check, and
`processNudges` then iterates both arrays unconditionally. One person, both arrays, **two SMS 500
milliseconds apart in a single run.**

**Proven against the live database, not inferred from reading the code.** `findNudgeCandidates()`
was run read-only on 2026-08-27 (`processNudges` never invoked) and returned
`eligibleFirst: ["Nudge Candidate Live"]` and `eligibleSecond: ["Nudge Candidate Live"]` — the
same person in both. That row is 10.0 days past its send with both stamps null, which is exactly
the state phase 2's verification catalogued. **Nothing has fired only because no SMS provider is
configured** — the same standing condition that keeps [[GTC-252]] latent.

The defect predates this ticket and predates [[GTC-178]]'s retime: the two-independent-`if`
structure is unchanged from the 24h/48h era, where anyone 48h past send with both stamps null hit
it identically. `dueNudgeIndices`' own docstring even anticipates the state — "`[0, 1]` means both
are (someone added late, or a cron that missed a tick)" — but nothing downstream ever capped it.

Filing note: this is fixed here rather than split out because Ruling 7(b) rules it directly and
phase 3 is already rewriting the two `if` statements in question. It is called out in the phase-3
commit as a bug fix in its own right, so it is not mistaken for cadence-control scaffolding.

### How the skip distinguishes don't-chase (executor's choice, Ruling 6)

**Chosen: the loop reads `PersonEvent.nudgeMark` directly and records the skip. The resolver's
return type is unchanged.** The two options were (a) the loop reads the mark, or (b)
`resolveNudgeOffsetDays` returns a richer result carrying WHY it returned `[]`. Reasons for (a):

- **It matches every existing skip.** The child rule, the token check, the phone check and
  opt-out all read a field off the row in the loop and call `addSkip`. A fifth skip that arrived
  through a different mechanism would be the odd one out for no gain.
- **A skip reason is a reporting concern, not a cadence concern.** `nudge-cadence.ts`'s own
  docstring draws that line already: "Whether a due leg should actually SEND is the caller's
  decision — opt-out, the child rule, response state and the already-sent stamps all live in
  `nudge-eligibility.ts` and none of them belong in a pure clock." A reason string is precisely
  that class of thing.
- **(b) breaks phase 1's pinned contract.** Eight assertions in `tests/nudge-cadence-test.ts`
  compare `JSON.stringify(resolveNudgeOffsetDays(...))` to a literal array, including the whole
  12-cell matrix. Changing the return type would rewrite the ruled matrix's proof to buy a reason
  string the sweep can read off the row for free.

**Consequence, stated so it is not mistaken for dead code:** the loop's mark check is a hard gate
that `continue`s, so the resolver's `[]`-for-don't-chase is never reached from the sweep. It
remains correct, remains tested, and remains live for the host-facing clock (Hinge §6). It
becomes **belt and braces** — the same two-place treatment the child rule already gets in this
file, where the SQL excludes CHILD and the JS re-checks anyway. Both are asserted, and they fail
in the safe direction: if the two ever disagree, the row is suppressed, not sent to.

### The `OFF` question — recommended, then RULED (Ruling 11)

Ruling 6 asked whether an `OFF` event should also record a skip, and withheld the decision if it
changes the shape. **It does, so nothing is built for it in phase 3.**

**What changes.** Every skip today means *"this individual was excluded for an individual
reason."* An `OFF` event is ONE host decision about the whole event, so recording it per person
turns a 40-guest event into `{ reason: 'Event nudge pace is OFF', count: 40 }` — the data shape
(`{reason, count}[]`) is untouched, but the semantic shape is not: skips stop being per-person
facts. The alternative that preserves the semantics — a separate field on `EligibilityResult` —
changes the TYPE instead, and that type propagates through `NudgeRunResult.candidates.skipped`
into the cron route's response.

**Recommendation: record it, per person, with a distinct reason string.** The invisibility
argument behind Ruling 6 applies with equal force — an operator asking "why did nobody on this
event get nudged" deserves an answer — and a large count is informative rather than noisy:
"40 people skipped because this event's pace is OFF" is exactly the sentence being sought. One
code path, one place, one pattern.

~~**Not built pending that ruling.** Until then an `OFF` event's people fall through silently,
which is the status quo and is the very thing Ruling 6 objects to for don't-chase. Recorded
plainly rather than quietly left.~~

**RULED 2026-08-27 — Ruling 11 takes this recommendation.** Built in phase 5, with the semantic
cost stated at the definition site as the ruling required. The accompanying pending-count
disagreement is closed in the same commit.

**Ruling 12 — `OFF` suppresses the PROXY path too (2026-08-27, at the close).**

**Found by the executor while writing the close, and ruled in rather than filed.** Phase 5
had already been committed (`d01379d`) with this recorded under "Not built here" as a real
gap plus a recommendation to suppress. The founder took the recommendation and named the
cause: **Ruling 3 covered don't-chase on both paths, and Ruling 11 covered only the direct
path.** That asymmetry was the defect.

**The argument is identical to Ruling 3's and stronger.** An `OFF` event silenced on one
path and not the other fails for **every household on the event**, not one person. §10.3
calls the pace "an event-level sending decision", and an event-level decision that reaches

Implemented, not filed: same place and same ordering as the don't-chase suppression — after

**The mark is still checked before the pace, on both paths.** Both produce no nudge, so the
order decides only which reason is REPORTED, and the mark is the more specific fact — a
hosting judgement about that person which survives the host switching the pace back on,
where a household present only because of the pace returns the moment she does. Asserted on
both paths, because the two must not explain the same household two different ways.

⚠ **This does not bound the proxy loop.** [[GTC-252]] stands: a household with an unmarked
channel on a non-`OFF` event is still eligible on every 15-minute tick. Suppression removes
households from that loop; it does not put a limit on it.
