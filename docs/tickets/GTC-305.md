### Signal

**Expected**
Ruling Y creates a population the host has to know about: people whose ask goes out successfully
and who will never be chased, because the chase cannot text them. Ruling Y says they are hers from
the moment of send, and that they read red.

**Actual**
Nothing in the tree knows they exist.

- **The pre-flight does not show them.** `readAskPreview` in `src/lib/preflight/ask-preview.ts`
  routes every membership through `chooseAskRoute` and nothing else. `chooseChaseRoute` in
  `src/lib/eligibility/channel-chooser.ts` has **no caller anywhere in `src/`** — verified by grep
  at `99cb852`. So `HostListLine` is built purely from ask-routing, and a person who can be asked
  and cannot be chased produces no line at all.
- **The board does not show them.** `RED_REASONS` in `src/lib/glance/state.ts` holds three —
  `DECIDE_BY_EXPIRED`, `REVERSAL`, `EXHAUSTED_SILENCE` — and `deriveItemState` gives an unanswered
  row `AMBER` / `AWAITING_REPLY` for anyone who is not the host. A person nobody will ever chase
  therefore reads as chased, which is ruling J's *"the board lying to her"* in a second population.
- **A red without a reason has no why-line.** `whyLineFor` in `src/components/glance/strip.ts`
  renders only the reasons in `WHY_PRECEDENCE`, so a red carrying an unlisted reason renders with
  no why at all, against [[GTC-192]] Ruling 4. Ruling M already recorded this gap for the
  never-reachable; ruling Y widens who falls into it.

**Smallest failing case**
An adult with an email address and no phone number, holding one item. Open the pre-flight message
step: she is listed as an ordinary email recipient, and the host's list does not name her. Under
ruling Y she is the host's from the send.

### Why this is not already somebody's

- **Not slice 1's.** Slice 1 owns the chooser, and ruling Y's change there is one line —
  `chaseChannelOf`'s narrow-reading branch and its `ANCHOR(GTC-189)`, with the three `[D15-narrow]`
  assertions in `tests/channel-chooser-test.ts` moving with it. That is inside the module and
  anticipated at slice 1. It makes the chooser answer correctly; it shows nobody anything.
- **Not slice 3's, as built.** Slice 3 shipped the host's list from ask-routing and is committed.
  Consuming the chase chooser is new work on a built slice, which is why it has a ticket rather
  than an amendment.
- **Slice 7 is the nearest owner and does not cover it.** Ruling M filed *"the unreachable red,
  with a reason and why-line of its own"* against slice 7. That red is for a person who was never
  messaged. This is a person who **was** messaged, successfully. Whether they share one reason or
  need two is Unknown 1.

### Unknowns

1. **One reason or two.** Ruling M's red is for a person never messaged; ruling Y's is for a person
   messaged and never chaseable. Whether `RED_REASONS` gains one member or two is not ruled, and
   ruling M records that the name and words of its own are still open.
2. **What the strip says while she is waiting.** A person can be red-because-unchaseable and also
   reply an hour later. What the board does at that moment — the red clears, or it was never about
   the reply — is not ruled.
3. **Whether the pre-flight's two populations read as one list or two.** Slice 3's heading is
   *"Yours to handle"* and its line is *"Gather will not message these people."* The second half of
   that sentence is false for this population: Gather does message them.
4. **[[GTC-300]] changes who is in it.** `isValidNZNumber` in `src/lib/phone.ts` rejects `+61`, so
   every Australian number is in this population until that predicate is fixed.
