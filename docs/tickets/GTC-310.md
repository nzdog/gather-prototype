# GTC-310 — her hand, and what it is overruling

**Status:** Open — **filed, not scoped.** Filed 2026-09-15 | **Branch:** TBD
**Severity:** [ ] Critical [ ] High [x] Medium [ ] Low — proposed at filing; the founder's to set.
`severity_proposed` carries the proposal and `severity` stays empty until it is set.
Escalated (brand/trust risk): [ ] No

**Filed on the founder's instruction**, from [[GTC-189]] *Founder rulings — fourth pass (Nigel,
2026-09-15)*, ruling AD, which answered decision 19:

> DECISION 19 — LET IT SEND, BUT TELL ME WHAT I AM OVERRIDING. The by-hand reminder is me choosing,
> and a rule I made for the automatic case should not silently overrule my own hand.

---

### Signal

**Expected**
When the host presses the nudge button on a person the automatic chase would have handed over, it
sends — and she is told what she is overriding before or as she does it.

**Actual**
It sends, silently, and the rule it crosses is invisible.

`chooseManualNudgeChannel` in `src/lib/sms/manual-nudge-recipient.ts` returns `'sms'` only when the
number exists, passes `isValidNZNumber` and is not opted out; otherwise it returns `'email'`
whenever the person has an address, and `'none'` when they do not. So it emails:

- a person whose phone is **opted out** — against ruling O, *"a no to one channel is treated as a no
  to being chased"*;
- a person whose number the send **cannot use** — against ruling P;
- a person with **no phone at all** — against ruling Y, which hands that person over at the send.

The function returns a channel and no reason, so nothing downstream could tell her even if the
screen wanted to.

**Smallest failing case**
A guest with an email address who has texted STOP. Press the nudge button on her row. An email
goes, and nothing on the screen mentions the opt-out.

**Verified at `99cb852`.** `sendNudgeEmail` in `src/lib/email.ts` has exactly two callers:
`POST` in `src/app/api/events/[id]/people/[personId]/nudge/route.ts`, and `src/lib/wrap-up.ts` —
the thank-you, which is not a chase. `findNudgeCandidates` in `src/lib/sms/nudge-eligibility.ts`
filters `person: { phoneNumber: { not: null } }` in SQL and `sendNudge` in
`src/lib/sms/nudge-sender.ts` calls `sendSms` alone, and ruling Y retires the email leg slice 8
would have added.

**So this route is the sole remaining producer of a chase email in the product.** Two things follow:

- **[[GTC-296]] must not drop it.** Ruling Q's *"every email carries a way out"* narrows under
  ruling Y from *the invitation and both reminders* to the invitation plus the emails that actually
  exist — and this is one of them. *[2026-09-15, ruling AG — and the scheduler's reminders to ticked
  people come back alongside it. GTC-296 is corrected there.]*
- **⚠ Zone 7.** This route emails an opted-out person. Ruling AD lets the host override that **by
  hand**; it changes nothing about what opt-out means for anything automatic, and it is not an
  argument for widening Zone 7. [[GTC-301]] is the ticket for the two opt-out facts, and it is not
  this one.

### Scope

**Fix here:**
- `chooseManualNudgeChannel` returning a reason alongside its channel, so the caller can say what
  was crossed.
- `POST` in `src/app/api/events/[id]/people/[personId]/nudge/route.ts` carrying it.
- The host-facing surface that shows it.

**Do not touch:**
- All Do-Not-Touch Zones in `GATHER-BUILD-CONSTANTS.md`. **Zone 7 is live and this ticket does not
  enter it** — no change to `Person.smsOptedOut`, `SmsOptOut`, `OPT_OUT_KEYWORDS`, or to any
  automatic path's reading of them.
- **The channel choice itself.** Ruling AD is *let it send*. The channel this function picks does
  not change; what changes is that the host is told.
- The function's existing refusal to consult provider configuration — [[GTC-214]]'s ruling,
  recorded in its own docstring, that reachability is a property of the recipient.
