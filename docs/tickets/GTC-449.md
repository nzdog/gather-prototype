# GTC-449 - Deleting a sample copy lowers every limit that counts copies

> Filed by Claude Code on 2 Nov 2026 as a stub, from the founder's
> reading of GTC-448's correction 5 that day, confirmed from the code
> at 7e52998 (read only; no start-and-delete loop was run). Branch
> feat/demo-public. Planned and built on 3 Nov 2026 at 026d376; not
> committed. The sample does not go public (GTC-447) until this is
> committed.

## Why

Every limit on sample copies counts copy EVENTS, the rows a visitor
can delete herself (DELETE /api/events/[id] admits the event's host).
When she deletes her copy, it drops out of every count at once:
- src/lib/demo/start-limit.ts L129-138: her own copies in the last
  hour (3), counted as copy events whose account address ends in her
  tag.
- L140-141: everyone's copies in the last hour (30).
- L143-146: everyone's copies since today's NZ midnight (150).
- L148, through src/lib/demo/ai-cap.ts L42-51 (`demoAiUsedToday`):
  the day's AI total (100), the sum of `aiCallsUsed` over copy events
  started today. takeDemoAiCall reads the same total at L67.

The only other thing in the way of starting again is the 409 in
src/app/api/demo/session/route.ts L66-79, and it checks that a
`session` cookie is present, nothing more; a caller who drops the
cookie passes it.

So anyone can start a copy, make its three plans, delete it, and
start again, and no limit ever stops her: the per-visitor hour, the
all-visitors hour, the day and the day's AI total all forget the
copy she deleted. That defeats the cost bound the sample depends on.

## Scope (to plan, not decided)

- Options to plan:
  - (a) The visitor cannot delete her own copy. This changes what
    GTC-448 handles: a copy would then only ever leave through the
    clean-up's first pass, and GTC-448's second pass would have
    nothing to find.
  - (b) The limits count something that survives the delete. The
    copy's account rows stay until the clean-up (GTC-448), so the
    start counts could read accounts; the AI count needs a place that
    survives the delete too. This does not change what GTC-448
    handles.
- Which, and how, is for the plan.

## The plan (3 Nov 2026, for the founder's rulings; nothing built)

### Recommended: option (a), the delete refuses on a sample copy

Why: one guard at the one door closes all four counts together, the
AI total included, with no schema change, no migration, no new route
and no change to start-limit.ts or ai-cap.ts. A copy then leaves only
through the clean-up, which never takes today's copies (correction
5), so every count stays exact for the whole day. A visitor loses
nothing she needs: W58 already tells her the sample is deleted the
next day, and she can still remove any household or person she typed
(those deletes move no count).

Where your lean is right, and where it needs adding to:
- Right: (a) closes every limit at once and needs no schema change.
- "GTC-448's second pass then has nothing to find" is not quite so.
  It stays a safety net for a copy deleted by hand or by a script,
  and for any copy a visitor deleted on some database before this
  ships. Keep it and its checks (case I).
- But GTC-448's own suite drives the visitor's DELETE over HTTP (O1)
  and needs a change on purpose. Left as it is, O1 to O5 go red, the
  next clean-up step throws (it deletes Sam while the kept copy still
  points at him), the run stops, and O6 to O28, F1 and F2 are never
  asserted: 25 checks lost without a red (case I).
- "No real need to delete" holds for cost, but there is one need:
  privacy. Someone who typed real names may want them gone now. Under
  (a) she can still delete each household and person she typed; only
  the event waits for the next day. The words say what she can do
  (case E).
- The page as it stands answers the refusal with "Failed to delete
  event. Please try again.", which tells her to repeat something that
  will fail again. So the plan proposes a small page change (case F).

### Option (b), set out fairly

(b) keeps the delete and moves the counts onto rows that survive it.
- The three start counts could count copy ACCOUNTS (User rows whose
  address ends `@demo-copy.invalid`, or `<tag>@demo-copy.invalid`, by
  User.createdAt) instead of copy events. Each start writes exactly
  one account in its own transaction (copy.ts L103), and the
  clean-up's second pass takes an account only past
  `expiredBefore`, so today's accounts stay all day. No schema
  change. It changes start-limit.ts (its Counter type gains `user`)
  and moves test:demo-start-limit's checks that build counts from
  events (L2 and the global ones), so more readers move than under
  (a).
- The day's AI total has no row that survives the delete. The copy's
  `aiCallsUsed` goes with the event; its AuditEntry and PlanRevision
  rows go too (deleted by hand in the route). The account and Sam
  have no field for it. So (b) has three sub-options for the AI
  total:
  - (b1) a new column or table that survives the delete (for
    example a count on the copy account, or one row per plan made).
    A schema change and a migration: a STOP under this prompt.
  - (b2) refuse the delete only once the copy has made a plan
    (`aiCallsUsed > 0`). That is (a) for most copies, with the same
    refusal and words, plus (b)'s start-count change.
  - (b3) accept the gap: the day's 100 then no longer binds. The
    starts still bind at 150 a day, three plans each, so up to 450
    plans a day instead of 100, four and a half times the ruled
    bound.
- For (b): she can delete her copy at once; GTC-448's second pass
  stays the main way her rows go, exactly as built; GTC-448's O1 is
  unchanged.
- Against (b): it leaves the AI bound open (b3), needs a schema
  change (b1), or needs (a)'s refusal anyway (b2); and it changes the
  limit code GTC-443 proved, where (a) does not touch it.

### Seen, not changed: plans that count on no day (case K)

`demoAiUsedToday` sums only copies started since today's NZ midnight.
A copy started before midnight can still make plans after it, and
those plans count on no day: not on its own day (already past), not
on the next (it was started the day before).
- Only a copy started in the last two hours before midnight can do
  so: every AI route needs her session, which lasts two hours from
  the start (copy.ts DEMO_SESSION_HOURS). The clean-up takes such a
  copy only once it is two hours old, after its session is gone.
- Starts in those two hours: at most 30 an hour for everyone, so 60
  (fewer if the day's 150 or the day's AI total at the start stops
  them).
- Each copy makes at most 3 plans (its own allowance), so at most
  60 x 3 = 180 plans a night count on no day. They do not lower the
  next day's 100 either, so in the worst case one NZ day's window
  carries 100 counted plans plus up to 180 uncounted.
- On the founder's list; not changed here.
