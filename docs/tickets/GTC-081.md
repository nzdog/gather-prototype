### What was found

Four contact sources investigated against the eight research questions defined in the ticket. Full findings in `docs/tickets/GTC-081-spike-findings.md`. Headline conclusions:

- **Google People API** is the highest-leverage integration (dominant NZ consumer coverage via Gmail/Android) but is gated on Google's **sensitive-scope OAuth verification**, which is a 1–6 week calendar-time review process that blocks production launch. Dev effort is modest (~4–6 days) but the verification queue is the real bottleneck and must be started early.
- **Microsoft Graph (Outlook/M365)** is technically simpler — no sensitive-scope review queue — and takes ~3–5 days. Valuable for work/professional-event hosts but less impactful for consumer hosts. No launch blocker.
- **Apple iCloud** has **no legitimate OAuth path**. CardDAV with app-specific passwords is the only mechanism, which creates a material credential-storage liability and poor UX. Recommendation: do not build a v1 CardDAV integration. Serve iPhone hosts via Google OAuth (if they use Gmail) or CSV.
- **Browser Contact Picker API** is the cleanest win — purely client-side, no backend, no tokens, no verification, ~1–2 days to ship. But it **only works on Android Chromium browsers**. iOS Safari has had it behind an experimental flag for years and it is effectively unavailable for typical iPhone users as of 2026-04. Desktop browsers do not support it at all. It is an additive enhancement for Android, not a standalone solution.

**Recommended build order:** (1) Contact Picker API, (2) Google People API (start verification paperwork in parallel with build ticket writing), (3) Microsoft Graph, (4) Apple iCloud only if iPhone coverage is still a gap (probably not).

**Architectural constraint identified:** Any OAuth integration for contacts must be a data-scoped secondary flow with its own token storage (e.g. a new `OAuthConnection` table). It must not touch the magic-link auth, session cookies, or `AccessToken.scope` system (Do-Not-Touch Zones 1–3 in GATHER-BUILD-CONSTANTS.md). NextAuth cannot be dropped in as a primary-auth replacement.

**Privacy flag raised:** Contact import collects personal information about people who are not Gather users. NZ Privacy Act 2020 (principles 5 and 11) requires privacy-policy disclosure, retention limits (zero retention beyond the explicit import action), and encrypted-at-rest storage for any persisted OAuth refresh tokens. This should be reflected in the build ticket for whichever provider lands first.

**Six open questions for Nigel** raised in the findings report — covering launch timing, host segments, iPhone strategy, token-storage appetite, developer-account ownership, and privacy-policy update ownership. Product decisions are needed before a build ticket can be scoped cleanly.

### Known limitations

- **NZ-specific market data was not surfaced** by web search. Gmail-dominance and high iPhone penetration in NZ are drawn from global stats plus reasonable inference, not hard NZ consumer telemetry. If precise NZ numbers matter for the product decision, Nigel should consider a lightweight survey of existing users or Stats NZ data rather than relying on this spike's inference.
- **Google verification timeline** is a range, not a fixed number — reviews can take 1–6+ weeks depending on app complexity, scope count, and review queue load. The spike cannot predict the exact calendar time; the 1–6 week range is based on current Google documentation and commonly reported ranges, and should be treated as an estimate.
- **iOS Safari Contact Picker flag status** is correct as of 2026-04, but Apple could promote it to default at any WWDC — worth re-checking before committing to the "iOS coverage = 0" assumption in a build ticket.
- **iCloud CardDAV finding is a non-recommendation, not a technical blocker.** If Nigel decides the iPhone segment is critical enough to justify the UX and security cost, a separate spike should validate whether Apple has changed stance and whether a server-side proxy + encrypted credential storage could be made safe enough for NZ Privacy Act principle 11 compliance.
