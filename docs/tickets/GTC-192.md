### Context

Moment 4 spec §3: the four-second contract — closing it, Kate must know (a) is anything mine to
do, (b) is it moving. Green/orange/red as **ownership, not status**. Behind a tap: all actions
(remind, reassign, take over a silence) — looking never becomes operating. The assistant's one
plain message when a critical item is red. Explicitly refused: messaging/chat, countdown, activity
feed, the plan content itself, analytics/response-rate reporting, progress bars or percentages.

§10.8: **the grid is person-primary.** People are the boxes; items live inside the person. A
person holding items in different states shows the **worst colour**, with all items visible on
tap. "Is the lamb covered?" is answered by the badge (J2, GTC-193) and the assistant's message,
not by inverting the grid.

Today: the Moment 4 screen doesn't exist in code at all — confirmed across every territory in the
discovery report. This ticket is the first thing that builds it, so §10.8 fixes the target data
shape before any code is written: a person-keyed read API, not an item-keyed one.

## Design direction — chosen 2026-08-30

**Reference of record:** `docs/design/moment4-glance-reference.md`, with a standalone viewable
mockup at `docs/design/moment4-glance-mockup.html` beside it. Chosen with the founder on
2026-08-30 after eight mockup iterations, which the reference records along with the trade each
one made. **This ticket now aims at that reference.** It is cited here, not restated — read it
before building.

The screen in one paragraph: a grid of **neutral household cards**. Each card carries the
household name and one thin, full-width **colour strip per member**; the strip's tint is that
person's state, and the card itself stays quiet. **Red = yours. Amber = with Gather. Green =
settled. Neutral = not chased.** Above the grid, a **summary sentence** is the four-second
answer — *"3 need you. Gather is on 9. 28 settled."* **Unassigned critical items** render as an
**alert strip above the grid**, since they have no person and person-primary gives them no home.
*The alert strip is the reference's proposal and is **unruled** — see new open decision 6.*

**§10.8 survives; its geometry does not.** Person-primary is intact — states belong to people,
and a person holding items in different states still shows the worst colour. What changes is the
unit of layout: the box is the **household card**, the person is a **strip inside it**. The card
deliberately carries no colour of its own, so no household-level merge rule is needed. The
reference's own framing draws the data model: **card = channel, strip = person** — messages go to
households, states belong to people.

## Open decisions the reference surfaced — ALL EIGHT RULED 2026-08-30

*Recorded 2026-08-30 as open; ruled the same evening. The questions are left standing as the
audit trail and each carries its answer inline — see **Founder rulings — the glance screen**
below for the full text of each ruling.*

1. **Sort order.** Do red households float to the top-left, or do houses hold fixed positions?
   The reference leans fixed — strips are loud enough that red finds you anyway, and fixed
   preserves the learned geography that iteration 4 lost — but explicitly does not rule it.
   **→ RULED (Ruling 3): fixed positions, host's household first.** The board is a map, not a queue.
2. **Red terseness.** Bare red strip, or red strip with a why-line (*"Amelia — quiet after 2
   nudges"*)? And its amber counterpart, clock-lines (*"Sarah — deciding Fri"*). Green stays bare
   either way. The two variants below are the test; this decision is its outcome.
   **→ RULED (Ruling 4): reds carry their why; amber and green stay bare.** The amber clock-line
   is preserved as a variant to test, by explicit intent.
3. **Green fold at large events.** At large headcounts, does the settled field fold to a count or
   stay as strips — warmth against noise? Unruled. Note iteration 2's recorded trade: folding
   buys constant height at any headcount but buries family members.
   **→ RULED (Ruling 5): green never folds, at any event size.** The green wall of actual names
   *is* the shoulder-drop; folding turns people into arithmetic.
4. **The "was in, now out" red (Uncle Ray's fade).** §8.6 rules that green can un-happen and goes
   straight to Kate; it does not say how long that red persists. Does it stay red until Kate
   acknowledges it, or fade to settled on its own? The spec supplies the red-source, not the
   behaviour.
   **→ RULED (Ruling 6): red with its why, sticky until seen; plays last in the replay, no
   sparks; settles once played.** "Seen" means the replay played — no acknowledge button.
5. **Declined guests.** *"Out — can't make it"* reads as green/settled in the reference's mocked
   state table. Whether a **no** is rest, or something Kate should see differently, is unruled —
   and the state table itself marks that row "pending decision 5".
   **→ RULED (Ruling 7): OUT fades entirely, text included; NOT-CHASED keeps border and
   full-strength text.** OUT is **not** green — which supersedes that state-table row.
6. **The critical-items strip placement.** The reference renders unassigned critical items as an
   alert strip above the grid. **Proposal, not a ruling** — this is the decision [[GTC-187]]
   flagged downstream (reconciliation row 4). Both the placement and whether an alert strip is the
   right form at all are open.
   **→ RULED (Ruling 8): ruled IN — above the grid, criticals only, with a quiet "and N more
   unassigned" door.** Ordinary unassigned items stay the plan's and pre-flight's business.
7. **The arrival replay's last-seen record.** The replay needs a per-host "last seen" marker — the
   reference suggests a `lastViewedAt` on the host's relationship to the event — plus a diff of
   state changes since. **This is schema, and it is ruled rather than discovered: it belongs on
   this ticket's migration list.** **Stop Condition 7 ("DB schema migration required") fires at
   build time** — the executor stops and reports before writing the migration rather than
   proceeding. When it is subsequently authorised, rehearse it against a scratch database first,
   per the migration caveat in `GATHER-BUILD-CONSTANTS.md`. Field name, owning model, and whether
   the diff is stored or recomputed are all open.
   **→ RULED (Ruling 9): the record exists — when the host last viewed, on her relationship to
   the event.** Shape decided at build time under SC7 with founder sign-off. **Nothing about
   guests is tracked.** SC7 still fires; the shape is deliberately still open.
8. **Live updates while on screen.** The reference's *"live changes get the same flourish"* — a
   reply landing while Kate is watching — requires the screen to learn about changes while open.
   **Polling or push, undecided.** The reference hands this to GTC-192 explicitly as a build
   decision.
   **→ RULED (Ruling 10): polling, roughly every 20 seconds.** Push is a later upgrade that
   changes nothing structural. **Do not build websocket infrastructure for this screen.**

## ⚠ STANDING WARNING FOR EVERY PHASE 6 SLICE — READ BEFORE MEASURING ANYTHING

**`gather_dev` IS NOT A NEUTRAL BACKGROUND.** 25 of its 26 events sit in **exactly the shape Ruling
28's bug needed** — responses set with no ledger row behind them. **Any glance measurement taken
against it is taken against a stacked deck.**

**And any fixture that builds state directly, rather than through the route a guest actually takes,
can encode the bug it is meant to catch.** It did, twice, in one slice: 6c's layer-3 fixture wrote
an `ACCEPTED` assignment with no ledger row, so the spark it asserted came out of the very
assumption Ruling 28 removes — the bug reached the test meant to catch it, and survived two test
layers and three slices doing so. Then 6c's own walk fixture turned out not to be idempotent, and
reported a board different from the one it was thought to have measured.

**THE RULES, FROM HERE ON:**

- **Every glance fixture writes the rows the real route writes.** Where a guest's tap would write an
  `AuditEntry`, the fixture writes one — or better, drives the route, as the no-op double-flip
  fixture already does.
- **Every walk fixture is idempotent across runs.** Run it twice and diff the output before trusting
  a single number from it.
- **Re-measure against an untouched database** — the note at the head of slice 6a says this about
  the counts; this says it about the *shape* of the data, which is the half that hid a bug.
- **A NEGATIVE RESULT NEEDS A POSITIVE CONTROL ON THE SAME BUILD, OR IT IS NOT EVIDENCE.**
  *(Added at 6e's sign-off, 2026-09-10.)* 6e's central claim was a NON-event: under Ruling 30 a
  live spark fired and **no stamp went out**. *"The spark did not stamp"* is equally consistent
  with **sparks having stopped stamping altogether** — a broken build produces the same reading as
  a working one. So the walk ran the same board again on the same build with **one variable
  changed** (a fresh session, so the quiet debt started false): the spark fired and **one** stamp
  went out, and the mark moved. **The pair is the evidence; either half alone is worthless.** This
  is the same shape as 6b's vacuous *"the memory NEVER MOVES BACKWARDS"* — trivially true of a route
  that 404s — and as the differentials Ruling 14 and Ruling 25 are asserted with. **The tell is that
  the finding is an absence.** An absence is only ever measured against a presence you produced
  yourself, on the same code, minutes apart.
- **A SUITE THAT CANNOT REPORT ITS RED IS NOT A RED.** *(Added at 6e's sign-off, 2026-09-10.)*
  Ruling 30's first RED run did not fail — it **crashed**, at module scope, on
  `TypeError: LV.liveStampDecision is not a function`, and **236 passing assertions never
  printed**. A RED is a document: it names which assertions fail and, just as importantly, which
  pass and why. A fixture that throws destroys both halves and leaves nothing to justify. **Build
  every fixture that touches a not-yet-existing export inside a `try`** — the same reason `ok()`
  exists for the assertions themselves — so a missing module reads as a failed assertion rather
  than as a dead run.

*That is the lesson of slice 6c and it outlasts the slice.*

**AND ONE MORE, FROM SLICE 6e, ABOUT THE SHAPE OF AN ASSERTION RATHER THAN THE SHAPE OF A FIXTURE:**

> **AN ASSERTION THAT MATCHES TEXT *NEAR* THE THING, RATHER THAN THE THING, IS NOT A GUARD.**

6e wrote three and caught all three before taking its RED as evidence — but only because it looked,
and they are the same family as 6c's fixture and 6a's schedule:

| Written as | Satisfiable without the behaviour existing |
|---|---|
| *the live stamp fires for a spark* — the word `spark` **within 600 characters** of the POST | a file that stamps unconditionally two lines under a comment about sparks |
| *nothing stamps while hidden* — a visibility token **within 400 characters** of the POST | a comment; and **defeated** by a one-line `visible()` helper, which is the right code |
| *the done event is announced after the stamp* — the event named **below** the POST in the file | it matched the **import statement** at the top, and textual position is not runtime order |

**Each was replaced by the mechanism itself** — one counted call site with its literal condition,
the guard's own definition plus the call behind it, and `.finally(announce)`, which *is* the
ordering. **The tell is the word "near".** Proximity, character counts and line order are properties
of a file, not of a program; a guard has to name the thing it is guarding.

⚠ **AND THE SAME PASS FOUND A REGEX THAT WOULD HAVE FAILED FOR AN INNOCENT REASON** — a ban on
`.from` in the live island, which `Array.from(` trips. **A guard that fires on the wrong thing is
the same defect as one that never fires**, and it is discovered later, by someone who does not know
why it is there.
