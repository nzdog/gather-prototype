# GTC-286 — neither generate route asks what state the event is in

**Status:** Open — needs scoping when pulled | **Effort:** TBD

*Found during [[GTC-237]]'s investigation, 2026-09-12.*

## Signal

**Expected:** once invites are out and guests have started replying, the plan stops being
regenerable — or at least stops being regenerable without saying so.

**Actual:** `POST /api/events/[id]/finalize-plan` and `POST /api/events/[id]/regenerate-plan`
both check the caller's role, that the event exists, that `aiCallsUsed` is under the cap, and
that an `EventSetup` row exists. **Neither reads `Event.status` and neither reads
`Event.sentAt`.** A `CONFIRMING` or `FROZEN` event regenerates exactly as a `DRAFT` one does.

There is no lifecycle gate on the client either: `/plan/[eventId]/setup` has no status guard,
and the plan view's "← Back to event setup" reaches Step 1's Generate from any state.

**Smallest failing case:** send invites, let one guest accept, open `/plan/[id]/setup`, back to
Step 1, Generate.

## What [[GTC-237]] did and did not close

[[GTC-237]] made the delete item-level and added "has an assignment that is not `PENDING`" to
the preserve set, so a guest's answered item now survives a regeneration. **That narrows the
blast radius and does not close this.** Still replaced after the send: every generated item
nobody has answered yet — which, days after the send, is most of the plan and precisely the
part the outstanding asks refer to. A guest holding an SMS about an item that no longer exists
is the failure this ticket names.

`edit-item-frozen-block-test.ts` shows the codebase already has a notion of a frozen edit being
refused. The generate routes were never brought under it.

## Candidate directions, not a ruling

- Refuse outright past a lifecycle point, and say which point: `sentAt` is the send ([[GTC-168]]
  renamed it for exactly that reason) and is a sharper line than `status`.
- Or allow it and make the consequence visible — the count of asks that would be invalidated,
  shown before the call rather than after.
- Whichever: one predicate, shared by both routes and by whatever the client uses to disable the
  control, rather than three spellings of the same question.
