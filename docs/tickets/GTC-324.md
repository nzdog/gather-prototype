## ⚠ THE SENTENCE THAT LEADS THIS TICKET, AND IT IS WHAT EARNS THE SEVERITY

> **A TEXT STOP IGNORED HARMS ONE PERSON'S TRUST. AN EMAIL COMPLAINT IGNORED DEGRADES DELIVERY FOR
> EVERY HOST ON THE PLATFORM.**

Founder ruling on the severity and on where that sentence belongs:

> GTC-324 — High and the brand flag confirmed. **The delivery-reputation half is what earns it:** a text
> STOP ignored harms one person's trust, an email complaint ignored degrades delivery for every host on
> the platform. That is the sentence that should lead it.

**Why it is literally true and not a figure of speech.** Complaint rate is the number mailbox providers
use to decide whether a sending domain reaches anybody at all. It is measured per domain, and Gather
sends every host's invitations from **one** verified domain. So a complaint that Gather keeps mailing
into is not a mistake contained to that recipient: it moves the number that decides whether the next
host's invitations land in an inbox or a spam folder. **The victim of ignoring it is a host who has done
nothing and will never know.**

That is also why it is not simply Zone 7's email twin. Zone 7 protects a person; this protects a person
**and** the channel every other person depends on.

## The asymmetry, and it is the whole ticket

**On text, Gather has a STOP and honours it.** `SmsOptOut` and `Person.smsOptedOut` are a
**Do-Not-Touch Zone** — Zone 7 in `GATHER-BUILD-CONSTANTS.md` — because the consequence of getting it
wrong is legal as well as rude. `POST /api/sms/inbound` reads a reply, writes the opt-out, and every
sender checks it before sending.

**On email, Gather has nothing.** There is no opt-out column for email, no unsubscribe read, and
until [[GTC-289]] no way to learn that anybody objected at all. Phase 1 changed exactly one thing: the
provider's word for it is now readable.

`ResendOutcomeKind` has a `COMPLAINED` member, and phase 1 deliberately kept it **out of the bounce
family**, on the founder's ruling:

> THE THREE PROVIDER FAILURES STAY SEPARATE, and `complained` stays out of the bounce family. Your
> reason is the ruling: the provider states them separately, and **a complaint is a live address whose
> owner does not want mail, which is not a dead channel.** §7 is about a dead channel.

**So the kind exists, it is correctly separate, and nothing reads it.** That is the gap.

---

## ⚠ What a complaint actually is, because the word undersells it

In every provider's vocabulary, a complaint is a **spam report** — the recipient pressed the button
in their mail client, and the mailbox provider told the sender. It is not a bounce, not a silence, and
not a soft signal:

- **The address is fine.** Mail will keep being accepted and keep arriving.
- **The person has objected.** Not to this message — to being sent mail by this sender.
- **The sender is being scored on it.** A sender who keeps mailing complainants stops being delivered
  to everybody else too — **the reputation half, which is the sentence at the head of this ticket.**

---

## The question, in three parts

1. **Does a complaint stop the chase for that person?** Slice 8's nudges are the thing that would
   send again. `findNudgeCandidates` has no idea this happened.
2. **Does it stop everything, or only the chase?** A complaint on an ask is different from a
   complaint on a wrap-up thank-you, and Gather sends magic links to the same addresses — a sign-in
   link somebody asked for is arguably not the mail they objected to. ⚠ **But an opt-out that
   silently keeps sending "transactional" mail is the trick every bad sender uses**, and the
   distinction should be ruled rather than assumed.
3. **Is it per-person, per-event, or per-address?** `SmsOptOut` is keyed on the phone number, not the
   membership. The same reading here would be per-address and would reach every event that person is
   in, including ones they have not been invited to yet.

## ✅ RULED 2026-09-27, AT SLICE 8 — "NOT YET" BECOMES AN ANSWER

Three founder rulings, asked one at a time. **Built by [[GTC-189]] slice 8**, not by this ticket.

### Ruling 1 — a spam report counts as an unsubscribe from that event (parts 1 and 2)

A complaint writes the same per-event fact an unsubscribe click writes — [[GTC-296]]'s `EmailOptOut`
— so everything GTC-296 built applies to that event: the chase stops on every channel, the wrap-up
email does not send, and the by-hand nudge can still go by text with its notice (ruling AD). The
ground, in short: a spam report is a stronger stop than an unsubscribe click, honouring the click
while ignoring the report makes no sense, and ignoring it costs every other host.

### Ruling 2 — it reaches every event and every host, matching Resend (part 3)

**Resend has already decided this for the account.** Its documentation, read 2026-09-27: a spam
complaint (or a hard bounce) puts the address on a suppression list that applies to the whole Resend
team — *"skipped across all your domains and subdomains when sending transactional or Broadcast
emails"* — and a later send is accepted by the API and not delivered. So Gather records the block
against the person and stops treating that address as email-reachable **anywhere**: text if the
person has a usable mobile, otherwise the host's list. **Other hosts see neutral words only and never
learn of the report.** Without this, other hosts' invitations would appear to send and silently never
arrive.

### Ruling 3 — what the host whose invitation was reported sees — THE FOUNDER'S WORDS

> I can't email this address anymore. Their email reported your invitation as spam. Text them, or
> I'll put them on your list.

Ruled as typed, with one typo corrected (*"invition"*). **The middle sentence is shown only to the host
whose invitation was reported**; every other host sees the first and third sentences alone (ruling
2). *"Their email reported"* puts the report on the inbox rather than the person, so it stays true
when a filter, or someone else in a shared mailbox, pressed the button.

⚠ **For a person Gather can still text, the third sentence would be false** — Gather chases them by
text itself. The plan proposes that variant's words for the founder's ruling.

### What the rulings bring with them — named, not ruled

- **Sign-in emails.** Resend's block is team-wide, so a guest who is also a host cannot receive
  sign-in links after a complaint. The likely fix, sign-in mail from a separate Resend account, is a
  later decision. Filed as a follow-up at slice 8's close.
- **Hard bounces sit on the same block list.** The plan proposes whether the address-wide fact
  records them too — recommended: yes, for ruling 2's reason — and checks that ruling U's *"send
  again to the same address"* is offered only for temporary failures, because after a permanent
  bounce Resend will not deliver it.
- **Detection.** Whether a complaint arriving days later updates `last_event` on a message already
  `delivered` is [[GTC-323]]'s open question, and the webhook needs a public URL. A later send that
  comes back `suppressed` is a backstop. The plan states how a complaint is learned.

## ⚠ Why it is a question and not a fix

**Because the shape of the answer decides whether it needs a column, and a column is a migration.**

- Shape A — **read it and record nothing**: the poller notices a complaint and writes it onto the
  `OutboundMessage` row only. No new opt-out state. The chase does not change, and the answer to
  part 1 is "no".
- Shape B — **an email opt-out, mirroring Zone 7**: a new table or a `Person` column, checked by every
  email sender the way `smsOptedOut` is. ⚠ That is a migration AND it is the email half of a
  Do-Not-Touch Zone, so it is not the executor's to design.
- Shape C — **per-event withholding**: the complaint writes a `withheldWhy` on future rows for that
  person, so the board shows why nothing was sent. Reuses machinery that exists and does not reach
  other events.

**None of the three can be chosen from the code**, and two of them touch either a zone or the schema.

---

## ⚠ What a ruling here also has to say about the host

Hinge §7's rules are about what the host SEES. A complaint is the one delivery outcome where **the
truthful sentence is socially expensive**: *"Sarah reported your invitation as spam"* is accurate,
unhelpful, and possibly wrong about who pressed the button (shared mailboxes, aggressive filters).

[[GTC-192]] owns what the glance shows and this ticket must not pre-empt it. But the ruling on part 1
implies something on the board either way: if the chase stops, the host will notice that one person is
never nudged, and the absence needs a sentence.

## Unknowns

1. **Whether Resend reports complaints on the poll at all, or only on the webhook.** `last_event` has
   a `complained` member, so the poll can report it — but whether a complaint arriving days later
   updates `last_event` on a message already marked `delivered` is unobserved. [[GTC-323]].
2. **Whether Resend suppresses the address itself after a complaint.** The SDK has a whole
   suppression API (`AddSuppressionOptions`, `SuppressionOrigin`) and a `suppressed` last-event value,
   which suggests the provider may refuse later sends on its own. **If it does, Gather's answer to
   part 1 is partly made for it** — and shape A becomes much more defensible.
3. **How many complaints a Gather-shaped send actually produces.** An invitation from a friend is not
   marketing mail, and the rate may be effectively zero. That changes the priority and not the answer.
