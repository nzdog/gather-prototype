# GTC-331 — a guest who reported a message cannot sign in, and nothing says why

**Status:** Open — a stub, filed 2026-09-27 at [[GTC-189]] slice 8a's close, on [[GTC-324]]'s
*"What the rulings bring with them"*. Not scoped.
**Severity:** proposed High — the failure is a person locked out, silently.

---

### Signal

Resend puts an address on a suppression list that covers the whole Resend team after a spam
complaint or a hard bounce. A later send to it is **accepted by the API and never delivered**
(Resend's documentation, read 2026-09-27; GTC-324 ruling 2).

Gather sends sign-in links (`sendMagicLinkEmail` in `src/lib/email.ts`) from the same Resend
account as every guest email. So a guest who reported one invitation as spam, and who is also, or
later becomes, a host, can never receive a sign-in link. The request succeeds, the link is created,
and nothing arrives.

Slice 8a records the block (`EmailBlock`, Do-Not-Touch Zone 9) and keeps every **guest** email off
a blocked address. It deliberately leaves the account mail alone: GTC-296 ruling 6 exempts sign-in
and welcome mail from the opt-out, and a sign-in link refused by Gather would be the same lock-out
with a different cause.

### What is known

- Only `EmailBlock` knows the address is blocked. `sendMagicLinkEmail` does not read it (asserted in
  `tests/email-block-test.ts`, layer S).
- The sign-in flow is Do-Not-Touch Zone 2.
- The likely fix named at the ruling: sign-in mail sent from a **separate Resend account**, whose
  suppression list a guest complaint does not reach. That is a later decision, not this stub's.

### Unknowns

1. Whether Resend lets a team remove an address from its suppression list, and on what terms.
2. How often a Gather guest is also a host.
3. Whether the sign-in page should say anything to a person whose address is blocked — and what,
   given that ruling 2 forbids telling other hosts about a report.

### Scope

Not scoped. Zone 2 applies to any change to the sign-in flow.
