# GTC-283 — Three ledger violations the vocabulary could not express

**Status:** Open — **filed, not fixed. Nothing is repaired by this ticket.**
Filed 2026-09-12 | **Branch:** TBD | **Commit:** [pending] | **Effort:** low

**Severity:** [ ] Critical [ ] High [ ] Medium [x] Low — nothing is broken; the change
ledger is unreliable in three specific, bounded ways.

*Source: the ticket-state reconciliation of 2026-09-12 (`c7d30c2`, `47f121f`). Filed
rather than fixed, per the house rule that a pre-existing issue found in passing is not
the finding pass's to repair.*

---

## Why these three are one ticket

They are three shapes of one finding: **the change ledger carried violations that the
old status vocabulary had no way to state, so nothing could report them.**

`.claude/skills/gather-docs-and-writing/SKILL.md` documented `status:` as
`# observed values: closed | in-progress | open | deferred`. Under a *described*
vocabulary none of the three below is a defect — a synonym is just another observation,
and a missing `commit:` is not a violation of anything, because nothing was required.
`c7d30c2` replaced that with an enumerated, binding set and a required-field table. All
three became statable in the same commit, and a single pass over the frontmatter then
found them.

**Record how they were found, because it is the argument for the convention.** Nobody
audited the ledger looking for these. No reader reported them. They fell out of writing
the rule down — one `node` pass over 241 frontmatter blocks, checking each status
against its required fields, run to verify a *different* change. A vocabulary that can
be violated is a vocabulary that can be checked; the previous one could be neither.

The next time someone asks whether a convention change earns its keep, this is the
answer: it paid for itself inside one commit, before it had been applied to anything.

## Finding 1 — `GTC-192`'s frontmatter omits a commit its own body cites

`docs/tickets/GTC-192.md` carries:

```yaml
commit: 307c5cc, c49fe02, 8792080, c43672e, 40d457c
```

Five hashes. Its body names more — phase 7 shipped as `2901ac7`
(*"feat(GTC-192): phase 7 ruled and shipped — the reading panel, the door treatment;
GTC-192 CLOSED"*), and phase 6's five slices name `9a67899`, `c05cfde`, `1789f46`,
`c7cb78d`, `69ae5c7`, `66a2cd7`. None of those is in the frontmatter list.

**Why it matters now rather than abstractly.** `2901ac7` is one of the two hashes
[[GTC-193]] was closed against in `47f121f`, because that commit is where the
criticality badge actually landed. So a reader who checks GTC-193's evidence against
GTC-192's frontmatter finds a hash on one ticket that the other does not admit to. The
body carries it; the queryable field does not.

Not fixed here. Whether the frontmatter `commit:` on a seven-phase ticket should list
every hash or only the closing one is a convention question this ticket does not
settle — but the two records disagreeing is a defect either way.

## Finding 2 — twenty-four `closed` tickets have no `commit:`

The table `c7d30c2` added makes `commit:` a requirement of `status: closed`. Twenty-four
tickets violate it:

```
GTC-031  GTC-032  GTC-038  GTC-042  GTC-043  GTC-044  GTC-045  GTC-046
GTC-049  GTC-081  GTC-081-spike-findings  GTC-082  GTC-083  GTC-089
GTC-101  GTC-133  GTC-136  GTC-141  GTC-145  GTC-200  GTC-202  GTC-208
GTC-FM1  GTC-FM3-FM5
```

Reproduce:

```bash
node -e '
const fs=require("fs"),p=require("path"),y=require("js-yaml");
for (const f of fs.readdirSync("docs/tickets").filter(f=>f.endsWith(".md")).sort()) {
  const s=fs.readFileSync(p.join("docs/tickets",f),"utf8");
  if(!s.startsWith("---\n")) continue;
  const d=y.load(s.slice(4,s.indexOf("\n---\n",3)+1))||{};
  if(d.status==="closed" && !d.commit) console.log(f);
}'
```

**Two of them have it nowhere in the ticket at all.** Spot-checked GTC-133 and GTC-200:
both say `**Commit:** [pending]` in the body as well, so neither record carries the
hash. GTC-200's is `e8e2ddd` — recoverable only because [[GTC-203]] happens to quote it
in prose. GTC-133's was not searched for.

This is the same defect as the empty `commit:` fields on the shipped Hinge tickets that
the 2026-09-12 reconciliation corrected, aged four months longer and on tickets nobody
has reason to re-read. The skill's own backfill pattern is the remedy shape; applying it
needs the hashes found first, one ticket at a time, from `git log --grep`.

⚠ **Do not backfill a hash you have not verified names that ticket's work.** The
reconciliation found two tickets (GTC-137, GTC-142) whose commits exist and whose work
is *gone* — a hash in `commit:` asserts the work is in the tree. See [[GTC-223]] on why
that distinction is load-bearing.

## Finding 3 — seven tickets at `status: fixed`, a synonym with no rule behind it

`fixed` is not in the enumerated set and never had a definition. It means `closed`:

```bash
grep -l "^status: fixed" docs/tickets/*.md
```

All seven are closed in substance. The interesting part is not the synonym, it is that
`fixed` existed for months as a live status value, was invisible to
`grep -l "^status: closed"`, and nothing could call it wrong — because the schema said
"observed values", and `fixed` had been observed.

## Scope

**Fix here:** nothing. This ticket is filing only.

**When pulled, three decisions, none ruled:**

1. Whether frontmatter `commit:` on a multi-phase ticket lists every hash or the closing
   one — then make GTC-192 agree with itself either way.
2. Whether the twenty-four get backfilled, annotated as unrecoverable, or exempted by a
   dated carve-out in the skill. Backfilling needs a verified hash per ticket and is the
   only option that leaves the rule true.
3. Whether the seven `fixed` tickets are rewritten to `closed`, or `fixed` is added to
   the set as an alias. The first is the point of having a set.

**Do not touch:**

- All Do-Not-Touch Zones in `GATHER-BUILD-CONSTANTS.md`.
- The enumerated status table in `.claude/skills/gather-docs-and-writing/SKILL.md`. It
  is correct as written, and the three findings above are violations of it, not
  arguments against it. **Do not widen the rule to make the backlog compliant** — a
  doctrine that bends to fit the backlog measures nothing, which is the sentence that
  commit put in the file.
- [[GTC-223]]'s five registry rows. Adjacent, separately scoped, different file.

## Acceptance

To be defined when scoped. At minimum: the three decisions above recorded with reasons,
and the verification pass re-run to zero violations for whichever findings the ruling
says to repair.

## Stop Conditions

Standard eight from `BUG-TICKET-TEMPLATE.md`. Additionally: if backfilling a hash
requires asserting that work is in the tree without checking, **stop** — that is the
defect [[GTC-137]] and [[GTC-142]] exist to warn about.
