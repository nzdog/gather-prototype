# GTC-448 - A copy deleted by its visitor is never cleaned up

> Filed by Claude Code on 1 Nov 2026 as a stub, from the founder's
> ruling on GTC-442's question 3 that day. Branch feat/demo-public.
> Planned, not built. The sample does not go public (GTC-447) until
> this is fixed.

## Why

GTC-442's clean-up finds an expired copy by its event: a sample event
whose host's account is on the made-up copy domain. A visitor can
delete her own copy (DELETE /api/events/[id] admits the event's host).
Once she does, the clean-up never finds it again, so her demo-copy
account, the host Person "Sam", her session (until it expires, and its
row after), and every person she typed (each stamped with the deleted
copy's id, GTC-441) stay in the database for good. W58 tells her
"Only you can see your sample. It, and everything you type into it, is
deleted the next day." For her that is untrue.

## Scope (to plan, not decided)

- Options to plan: a second pass in the hourly sweep that finds
  demo-copy accounts and stamped people whose copy event no longer
  exists, past the same age as GTC-442's rule, with the same skip
  guards (a WrapUpLink; a person with a membership on another event; an
  account that is not a copy's made-up address).
- How "past the same age" is read once the event row is gone (the
  account's or the people's own creation time) is for the plan.
- W58 stays as approved.

## Zone stops

### The orphan rule

An ORPHAN ACCOUNT is a User whose address ends @demo-copy.invalid
(the made-up copy address; the crowd's addresses end
@<hex>.demo-copy.invalid and are not accounts), created before the
age cut-off below, with no EventRole, and none of whose people hosts,
co-hosts or belongs to (PersonEvent) any event. It is found by the
account, never by a person: the account is the only row that still
carries a clock (correction 1).

Its people, collected inside its transaction:
- its own: every Person whose userId is the account (Sam);
- the stamped: every Person whose sampleEventId is one of its own
  people's marks, and only when no Event has that id. If an Event
  with that id exists, the account is skipped ("its copy still
  exists"), so a live copy's people are never collected through a
  wrong mark.
- An orphan whose Sam carries no mark (a copy made before GTC-441)
  collects Sam only.

### The age rule, with no event row

The account's own createdAt, against the same expiredBefore(now):
created before today's NZ midnight AND more than two hours ago. The
account is written in the copy's own transaction (correction 6), so
its createdAt is the copy's start, and the rule means for her what it
means for a copy. Her two-hour session has run out by then.

At the edge: a visitor who starts at 21:30 NZ and deletes at 21:31
goes at the 00:17 run (the first run past both midnight and 23:30);
one who starts at 23:00 and deletes at once goes at 01:17. Any start
goes on the next NZ day, never the same day, as W58 says. A copy
being started now cannot be caught: it is young, and its account is
never seen without its event.

### The guards

- An address not a copy's: skipped ("not a copy account"); the find
  never selects one, and the unit re-reads it.
- A WrapUpLink naming any collected person: the whole account is
  skipped ("a wrap-up link exists").
- A collected person who belongs to another event is kept, and the
  rest goes. "Belongs" here reads a membership (as GTC-442), and also
  hosting or co-hosting an event (see case K).
- The rule is read again inside the unit's transaction: an account
  that is gone, young, or back on an event is "gone" (not counted,
  uses no place), as GTC-442 treats a copy already gone.

### The order of deletion, per account, one transaction

StructureTemplate and QuantitiesProfile rows keyed on the account or
its own people; then the collected people not kept; then the
account, whose cascade takes its Session rows (and EventRole and
Subscription rows, of which it has none). A unit that throws rolls
back whole, is counted failed, and the run goes on.

### The limits

The second pass has its own: up to 100 orphan accounts looked at
(DEMO_CLEANUP.lookAt), at most 25 deleted (DEMO_CLEANUP.perRun),
oldest first by createdAt then id; a skipped or failed account uses
no place (case L). The sweep's `limit` and `lookAt` options apply to
each pass alike.

### The route and the counts

The counts gain four keys, orphansFound, orphansDeleted,
orphansSkipped, orphansFailed; found, deleted, skipped and failed
stay the copies'. people, peopleKept, accounts, sessions and
templates count both passes. The route's body carries them as it
carries the rest (`...counts`). The route answers 500 when the sweep
throws, a copy failed, or an orphan account failed; 200 otherwise
(case M).

### The log

Counts only, never a name, an address or an id. The one summary line
per run keeps its opening words and gains the orphans' four counts:
"[demo-cleanup] copies found N, deleted N, skipped N, failed N;
orphan accounts found N, deleted N, skipped N, failed N; people
deleted N, kept N; accounts N, sessions N, templates N". One line per
skip, kept person or failure: "skipped an orphan account: <why>",
"kept N person(s) of an orphan account: they belong to another
event", "an orphan account could not be deleted (<code>)".

make that state.
