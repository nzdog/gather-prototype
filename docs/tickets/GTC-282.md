## Signal

**Expected**
A single-use sign-in link expires on the timescale of the sign-in it serves.
The tree has a house value and it is **15 minutes**.

**Actual**
`sendWelcomeEmail` in `src/lib/email.ts` mints its `MagicLink` with a
**30-day** expiry:

```
const expiresAt = new Date(Date.now() + 30 * 24 * 60 * 60 * 1000); // 30 days
```

Its own email copy says so out loud — *"You're currently logged in, but save
this link to return anytime"* and *"This link expires in 30 days."*

The other two minting sites both use 15 minutes:

- `POST` in `src/app/api/auth/magic-link/route.ts` —
  `new Date(Date.now() + 15 * 60 * 1000)`
- `POST` in `src/app/api/auth/claim/route.ts` —
  `new Date(Date.now() + 15 * 60 * 1000) // 15 minutes`

`POST` in `src/app/api/auth/verify/route.ts` consumes all three identically:
`findUnique` on `token`, reject if `expiresAt < new Date()`, reject if
`usedAt` is set, then mint a 30-day `Session`. **There is no per-link policy
at the consumption end** — the expiry chosen at mint time is the whole of it.

**Smallest failing case**
Create an event through the paid flow. The welcome email carries a
`/auth/verify?token=…` URL that will still sign anybody in a month later.

---

## Why it is being filed now rather than left alone

Today the link is a convenience. The host already has a live session when it
is sent — the email itself says so — so the link is a bookmark, and a stale
bookmark in your own inbox is a small thing.

[[GTC-280]] changes what it is. Under that fix `POST /api/events` stops
minting a session, and a link mailed to the paid address becomes **the only
way a signed-out host reaches the event she just bought.** A courtesy becomes
the front door.

Two things follow, and only the second is a defect:

- **Load-bearing.** If [[GTC-280]] keeps using `sendWelcomeEmail`, this link's
  expiry is a product decision about how long someone has to open their new
  event, not an afterthought. 15 minutes may be too short for that and 30 days
  is certainly too long; the right answer is neither of the existing two and
  wants deciding on purpose.
- **A month-long bearer credential in a mailbox.** Single-use, but unused
  until used. Anyone who reaches that inbox in the following month — a shared
  family computer, a synced tablet, a forwarded thread, an old device, a
  mailbox breach — signs in as the host and, through `POST /api/auth/verify`,
  receives a fresh 30-day `Session`. Chained, one email grants two months.

**Nothing revokes it.** There is no session list and no bulk revocation
([[GTC-280]] finding 4), and there is no way to invalidate an outstanding
`MagicLink` short of consuming it or deleting the row.

## Scope — PROPOSED, not ruled

**This is Do-Not-Touch Zone 2 and that is the point of the ticket.** Zone 2
protects magic-link generation, expiry and consumption. Changing an expiry is
precisely the thing it exists to guard, which is why [[GTC-280]] would not do
it in passing.

Candidate shapes, none chosen:

- **Match the house value.** 15 minutes, one constant, three sites agree.
  Simplest, and hardest on a host who pays then closes her laptop.
- **A named middle value for the post-payment link**, e.g. 24 hours, defined
  once as a constant with the reason written down. Two policies, both
  deliberate, and the constant is the place the reason lives.
- **Split the instrument.** A short sign-in link plus a separate durable
  bookmark that is *not* a credential — a plain `/plan/<id>` URL that requires
  signing in. This is the only shape where "save this link to return anytime"
  stays honest, because the thing being saved stops being a key.

**Whichever is chosen, the copy must change with it.** The email currently
promises 30 days in prose. A number in a template and a number in a `Date`
constructor that can disagree is its own small defect.

**Adjacent, do not fold in:** [[GTC-265]] covers whether these sends report
their own failure. Different defect, same function. This ticket is about how
long the link lives, not whether it was delivered.

## Acceptance — sketch

- [ ] All `MagicLink` expiries in the tree are traceable to a named constant
      with a written reason, and no expiry is a bare inline arithmetic
      expression.
- [ ] The expiry asserted in a test matches the expiry stated in the email
      copy, so the two cannot drift.
- [ ] `POST /api/auth/verify` still rejects an expired link and still rejects
      a used one — asserted, so the fix cannot weaken consumption while
      changing generation.
- [ ] A positive control in the same run: a fresh link still signs in.

## Unknowns

1. **How long a host actually needs** between paying and opening the event.
   A product question, and the one that decides this ticket.
2. **Whether [[GTC-280]] ends up using `sendWelcomeEmail` at all**, or sends a
   purpose-built sign-in link instead. If the latter, this ticket narrows to
   the leftover courtesy link and its severity drops.
3. **Whether any outstanding 30-day links exist in the database today.**
   Checkable; not checked, because reading `MagicLink` rows is reading live
   credentials and there was no reason to at filing time.
