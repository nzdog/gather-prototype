# GTC-321 — the act the press replaced, and never ruled on

**Status:** Open — **filed 2026-09-19, not scoped. It is a product ruling before it is a build.**
**Branch:** TBD
**Severity:** [ ] Critical [x] High [ ] Medium [ ] Low — proposed at filing; the founder's to set.
`severity_proposed` carries the proposal and `severity` stays empty until it is set.
Escalated (brand/trust risk): [ ] No — nothing is wrong for a guest today. It is High because it
governs whether a population of guests is chased at all, and because the surface it lives on is the
host's main planning screen.

**Filed on founder instruction, 2026-09-19**, out of [[GTC-189]] slice 5c's shape proposal. The
founder's words at the filing:

> FILE C AS THE QUESTION IT IS. Your finding is the whole ticket: after rulings AC and G, Gather is
> the sender, so "I sent them myself" is a different act the model has never ruled on — and it has a
> consequence, because a hand-sent person has no provider acceptance, so no `PersonEvent.sentAt`, so
> `findNudgeCandidates` never chases them. Recording a hand-send honestly means ruling whether those
> people are chased at all.

---

## The question, in one sentence

**Does "I've sent the invites myself" survive as an act, now that pressing send makes Gather the
sender — and if it does, is a person who was sent to by hand ever chased?**

---

## Why it is a question at all

**The workflow is older than the press, and nothing has ruled it dead.**

Before [[GTC-189]] there was no sender. The host shared the invite links herself — copied them,
texted them, read them out — and then told Gather she had, so that the tracking could start. That is
what `POST /api/events/[id]/confirm-invites-sent` was named for, and what its two buttons still say:
**"I've sent the invites"**, on the host's main planning screen and on the host magic-link view.

Two [[GTC-189]] rulings then made Gather the sender:

- **Ruling AC** — the press refuses an event whose host has no account, *"because replies would have
  nowhere to go"*. A refusal about reply-to only makes sense for a message Gather sends.
- **Ruling G** — *"each person's clock starts when their provider accepts their message"*. A clock
  anchored on provider acceptance only exists for a message a provider accepted.

**So the act the buttons name is no longer the act the route performs.** Slice 5a renamed the route
to `/send` (decision 31) for exactly that reason: *"a route named for a behaviour it no longer has is
the same defect class as everything else this week."* What it did NOT do is decide whether the
behaviour the old name described should still be available under some other name.

⚠ **AND [[GTC-189]] SLICE 5c's SECOND HALF TAKES THE BUTTONS AWAY WITHOUT ANSWERING THIS.** Founder
ruling, 2026-09-19: **option D** — both buttons become *"Review and send"* and navigate to the
pre-flight instead of pressing. The reason recorded there:

> The pre-flight exists to be the one screen where she sees what the press will do before doing it,
> and three pressing surfaces is the thing it was built to prevent. D removes the danger without
> deciding the workflow, and it is reversible if C is ever ruled in.

**D deliberately does not foreclose this ticket.** Nothing is deleted; two buttons stop pressing and
start navigating. If a hand-send act is ruled in, it gets its own affordance and its own words, and
neither is taken by what D did.

---

## ⚠ The consequence that makes it more than a naming question

**A hand-sent person has no provider acceptance, so no `PersonEvent.sentAt`, so they are never
chased. Ever.**

The chain, each link verified in the tree:

- Ruling G moved `PersonEvent.sentAt` off the press and onto acceptance. [[GTC-189]] slice 5a deleted
  the `personEvent.updateMany` that used to stamp it, in both press routes.
- `findNudgeCandidates` in `src/lib/sms/nudge-eligibility.ts` filters `sentAt: { not: null }` and
  records why in its own comment: *"A FAIL-SAFE, NOT A TIDY-UP. No personal send clock means the
  system does not know when this person was told, so it must not guess."* It then anchors the whole
  cadence on `membership.sentAt!`.
- So a person Gather never sent to has no clock, and a person with no clock is not nudgeable —
  **which is correct for someone who was never reached, and is the open question for someone the
  HOST reached.**

⚠ **Slice 5a already carries the same consequence for a WITHHELD or REJECTED recipient, and accepted
it:** *"a WITHHELD or REJECTED recipient never gets a `PersonEvent.sentAt`, so they are never chased,
ever. That is correct — nothing chases someone who was never reached."* **A hand-sent person is the
case where that reasoning stops applying**, because they WERE reached — just not by Gather.

**So the ruling has three possible shapes, and they are not equivalent:**

1. **No hand-send act.** The press is the only way anyone is told. A host whose guests Gather cannot
   reach handles them entirely off the board, as ruling M's red already says (*"a person nobody can
   reach is the host's from the first moment"*). Simplest, and it loses the tracking a host used to
   get for people she contacted herself.
2. **A hand-send act that records the contact and starts no clock.** Honest about what Gather knows,
   and the people it covers are never chased — which is the status quo of shape 1 with a record
   attached.
3. **A hand-send act that starts the clock.** Then those people ARE chased, by text or email, on
   Gather's cadence, about a message Gather never sent and whose contents it does not know. ⚠ That
   reaches Hinge §5's *"continuity by declaration"* — a chase that says *"I'll check back if I
   haven't heard from you"* about an ask Gather did not make — and it reaches Zone 7, because the
   chase would text a number Gather has no record of having used.

**None of the three is obviously right and the executor is not choosing.**

## ⚠ AND IT IS COUPLED TO `Person.inviteAnchorAt` — ONE RULING, TWO QUESTIONS

*Recorded here rather than as its own ticket, on founder instruction, because the coupling is the
point.*

[[GTC-189]] slice 5a raised `Person.inviteAnchorAt` as a loose end: the press still writes it, while
`PersonEvent.sentAt` moved to acceptance, so the two now disagree by the drain window. Three surfaces
read it — the plan page's `daysSinceAnchor`, `GET /api/h/[token]`'s `else if (person.inviteAnchorAt)
status = 'SENT'`, and the invite-detail route.

**The obvious tidy-up is to move `inviteAnchorAt` to acceptance as well, so the two clocks agree.
DO NOT DO THAT WITHOUT RULING THIS TICKET FIRST.**

⚠ **`inviteAnchorAt` is what makes button 1 disappear.** Found by checking rather than assuming, at
slice 5c's button report: `GET /api/events/[id]/invite-status` derives `NOT_SENT` from
`person.inviteAnchorAt`, not from `PersonEvent.sentAt`, and `InviteStatusSection` renders its button
only while `counts.notSent > 0`. The press still writes the anchor, so after a press every person
reads `SENT`, the count falls to zero, and the button goes away.

**Move the anchor to acceptance and that stops being true.** In an environment where no provider
accepts anything — which is this one, [[GTC-247]] — the anchor would never be written, the count
would never fall, and the host would face a **permanently visible button whose every press answers
409 `ALREADY_SENT`.** A tidy-up that makes the two clocks agree would make the screen lie in a new
way.

**So the ruling on a hand-send act has to say what `inviteAnchorAt` means.** It is currently the only
thing in the model that answers *"has this person been told, by anyone, by any means"* — which is
precisely the question a hand-send act asks. That may be a coincidence of history or it may be the
column the act already has.

## What exists today, for the record

- **`POST /api/events/[id]/send`** and **`POST /api/h/[token]/send`** — the press ([[GTC-189]] slice
  5a, decisions 30 and 31). The old `confirm-invites-sent` paths survive as a 307 in
  `next.config.js`.
- **`InviteStatusSection`** on `/plan/[eventId]`, reachable by a signed-in HOST `EventRole` holder,
  shown while the event is CONFIRMING and `counts.notSent > 0`, labelled *"I've sent the invites"*
  under *"After sharing the invite links, confirm below to start tracking."*
- **The host view at `/h/[token]`**, reachable by anyone holding the HOST magic-link token — no
  session required — shown while `Event.sentAt` is null, same label.
- ⚠ **Both become *"Review and send"* and navigate to the pre-flight at [[GTC-189]] slice 5c's second
  half.** After that, nothing in the product performs a hand-send record, and nothing says it used to.

---

## ⚠ What is NOT the question here

- **The second press.** Closed at slice 5a by GTC-169's guard: `pressSend` refuses `ALREADY_SENT`
  with a 409 once `Event.sentAt` is set, and both buttons are hidden by then. Recorded because the
  first framing of the button danger put it here and that was wrong — **the danger was always the
  FIRST press**: one irreversible act, two surfaces, a button promising the opposite of what it does,
  and none of the pre-flight's five checks.
- **Whether the press should send at all.** Ruled. It sends.
- **The unreachable person.** Ruling M and the host's list already own them. This ticket is about a
  person Gather COULD have reached and the host reached first.

## Scope

**Decide here:**
- Whether a hand-send act survives, in which of the three shapes above.
- What `Person.inviteAnchorAt` means once it does or does not.
- If it survives: its words, its surface, and whether the people it covers are chased.

**Do not touch:**
- All Do-Not-Touch Zones in `GATHER-BUILD-CONSTANTS.md`. **Zone 7 is live for shape 3** — a chase
  about a message Gather did not send would text a number on no record of use.
- `findNudgeCandidates`'s `sentAt: { not: null }` filter. It is a fail-safe by its own comment and
  changing it to admit a clockless person is the whole of shape 3, not a detail of it.
- Ruling G. The press's own clock is settled; this is about a clock the press did not start.

---

## Unknowns

1. **Has any real host ever used the hand-send flow?** Unmeasured. `Person.inviteAnchorAt` is
   written by the press as well, so its presence does not distinguish a hand-send from a press —
   which means the question may not be answerable from this database at all.
2. **Does a hand-send need a channel?** Shape 3 has to chase by something, and Gather would be
   guessing which channel she used.
3. **What the board shows.** A hand-sent person who is never chased is not amber (nothing is
   chasing) and not red (nothing failed). That is ruling J's problem in a new place, and [[GTC-305]]
   is adjacent.
