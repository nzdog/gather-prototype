## ⚠ THIS TICKET NOW BLOCKS [[GTC-280]] — added 2026-09-12

**Founder ruling, 2026-09-12:** *"[[GTC-265]] is the Resend false-success
ticket and it has been ready since before Moment 4 started. It becomes a

[[GTC-280]] removes the automatic session mint from `POST /api/events`, so a
signed-out host who has just paid reaches her event **only** through an
emailed sign-in link. That makes a silently-failed Resend send the difference
between "she pays and gets in" and "she pays and is told to check an inbox
nothing was sent to". [[GTC-280]] does not start until this lands.

**⚠ AND THE PART [[GTC-280]] NEEDS IS THE PART THIS TICKET OFFERS TO CUT.**
The Scope section below offers to narrow back to `sendNudgeEmail` alone if the
widening to four call sites is unwanted. **That cut would leave [[GTC-280]]
blocked**, because the site it depends on is whichever sender carries the
post-payment link — `sendWelcomeEmail` today — and that is one of the three on
offer to drop. If the scope is narrowed, it must still include the
post-payment sender.

**The contract [[GTC-280]] needs, stated here so it can be built to:**

> The sender **returns** its result and **does not decide** what to do about a
> failure. Each caller decides. `POST /api/auth/magic-link` goes on answering
> `{ ok: true }` byte-identically whatever happens, because that is
> enumeration protection and it is correct. `POST /api/events` must fail
> loudly, because the payer typed the address herself and has just been
> charged for it, and there is nothing to enumerate.

Unknown 1 below asks "where should a failed auth send be recorded" as one
question. **It is three questions with three different right answers**, and
the post-payment one is the easy one: it is not enumeration-sensitive, so it
is answered in the response.

## ⚖ THE CENTRAL RULE — return, do not decide (ruled 2026-09-12)

**This is the ticket's central rule, not an implementation note.** It is
recorded here, and it must also be written into the header of
`src/lib/email.ts` so the next person meets it at the senders rather than in a
ticket.

> **A sender RETURNS its result and does not DECIDE what to do about a
> failure. Each caller decides.**
>
> `POST /api/auth/magic-link` and `POST /api/auth/claim` stay byte-identical
> whatever happens, because that is enumeration protection and it is correct.
> The post-payment send fails loudly in the response, because she typed the
> address herself and has just been charged for it, and there is nothing to
> enumerate.

**What earns the rule** is Unknown 1 below. It asks "where should a failed
auth send be recorded" as one question. **It is three questions with three
different right answers**, and a sender that picks one answer for all of its
callers forces [[GTC-280]] to reopen a Zone 2 file to undo the decision. Cheap
now, annoying later.

Founder ruling, 2026-09-12: *"Take it, and record it as the ticket's central
rule rather than an implementation note… Write the rule in the sender's own
header so the next person meets it there."*

**One refinement, stated so it is not read as a loophole: RECORD inside,
DECIDE outside.** A sender may log a failure server-side — that is a record,
and Unknown 1 asks for one. What it may not do is choose the caller's status
code, swallow the failure, or throw in place of returning.

## Signal

**Expected**
When Resend rejects a send, the caller learns it: `sendNudgeEmail` in `src/lib/email.ts` returns
`{ success: false }`, `POST /api/events/[id]/people/[personId]/nudge` answers 502 as it already does
for the SMS leg, no `NUDGE_SENT_HOST` is logged, and no cooldown starts.

**Actual**
Nothing is sent and the host is told it was. Measured 2026-09-01 against the configured
`RESEND_API_KEY`:

```
resend.emails.send(…) →
  {"data":null,"error":{"statusCode":401,"name":"validation_error","message":"API key is invalid"}}
```

**The Resend SDK RETURNS its error rather than throwing it.** `sendNudgeEmail` awaits the call inside
a `try`, never looks at the result, and returns `{ success: true }`. The route then:

1. answers **200**;
2. calls `logInviteEvent` (`src/lib/invite-events.ts`) to write **`NUDGE_SENT_HOST`**, with
   `contactMethod: 'email'`;
3. starts the 24-hour window that `COOLDOWN_MS` in that route enforces — so **the retry of a message
   that never left is refused with 429** for a day.

The host's surface says `Reminded.` The audit trail says it was sent. Neither is true.

**Smallest failing case**
With any invalid or revoked `RESEND_API_KEY`, nudge an email-only guest. The UI reports success and
an `InviteEvent` of type `NUDGE_SENT_HOST` appears.

**⚠ WRONG IN ANY ENVIRONMENT, NOT A DEV-CREDENTIALS ISSUE.** [[GTC-247]] records that no provider
authenticates in local dev, and that is what made this visible — but the defect is not about the key
being wrong. A **production** key revocation, a suspended domain, a rate limit, a bounce rejected at
submission, or a Resend outage all arrive by the same door: a returned `error`, unread. Production
would report every one of them as sent.

**Contrast, from the same walk: the SMS leg is honest.** `sendSms` in `src/lib/sms/send-sms.ts`
returns `{ success: false, blocked: 'SMS_DISABLED' }`, the route answers **502**, and **no**
`NUDGE_SENT_HOST` is written. The two legs of one route disagree about whether a failed send is a
failure.

| Leg | Provider result | Route | InviteEvent | Cooldown |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SMS | `{success:false, blocked:'SMS_DISABLED'}` | **502** | none | not started |
| Email | `{data:null, error:{401,…}}` | **200** | `NUDGE_SENT_HOST` | **started** |

## Unknowns

1. **Where a failed auth send should be recorded.** `InviteEvent` is scoped to an event and a
   person; a magic-link failure has neither. A log line may be the whole of it, or it may want a
   row somewhere. Unresolved, and it is the reason the auth sites are separable from the nudge fix.
2. **Whether a failed nudge should be recorded at all**, rather than merely not recorded as sent.
   `sendSms` writes `SMS_BLOCKED_INVALID` and `SMS_BLOCKED_OPT_OUT` rows for its own refusals, so
   there is a house precedent for a negative record — but no `EMAIL_FAILED` type exists in the
   `InviteEventType` enum, and adding one is schema. **If that is wanted, Stop Condition 7 fires.**
   Not adding one is a complete fix for everything above.
3. **Whether Resend distinguishes "rejected" from "accepted then bounced".** This ticket only closes
   the first. If a message accepted at submission can still fail later, that is the email half of
   [[GTC-264]] and it is a different ticket again — do not scope-creep into it here.

substitution had silently failed to apply. The code under test was unmutated.
A mutation that reports no failures has made **two** claims, not one: that the
assertion is weak, AND that the mutation applied. The second was false and was
believed anyway.

Re-applied properly, M3 failed three assertions. Both the phantom and the real
run are recorded because the phantom is the more useful of the two: it is
[[GTC-267]]'s rule ("an empty search result is a claim, and a claim needs a
control") arriving in a new place.

**Generalised out of this ticket at the founder's instruction and recorded as
[[GTC-277]] Finding 8**, beside Findings 6 and 7, because all three are the
same root — a summary signal accepted in place of the artefact it summarises.
The rule: *a mutation that reports no failures has two explanations, and they
are not the same fact. Verify the edit landed before reading its result.*

**The weakness M3 was wrongly credited with finding was nevertheless real, and
it was measured rather than reasoned about.** The original assertion matched
`/resend|email|send/i`, which **Resend's own internal `logError` satisfies** —
it writes `"[Resend API Error]:"` to `console.error`. A controlled run put the
original weak assertions back alongside the real mutation: **only one of the
three failed**, and that one was the new `NODE_ENV=production` assertion which
did not exist before. So two of three would have missed it.

Worse, and this is why the new assertion is shaped as it is: the SDK's log is
**suppressed when `NODE_ENV === 'production'`** — the assertion was strongest
in the environment that needs it least. The record is now asserted under the
sender's own `[Email] … REJECTED` prefix, and again under
`NODE_ENV=production` with Resend's log proven absent.
