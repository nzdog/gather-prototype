# GTC-187 — H2: Dual-voice message architecture

**Status:** Open | **Branch:** TBD | **Commit:** [pending] (filed `e5f6e82`) | **Effort:** medium

*Effort level is a proposal from the discovery-report ticket breakdown — run `/effort` manually
before execution.*

### Context

Moment 4 spec §8.4: the thank-you's voice is Gather's by default; Kate can override to her own, or
mix. The mix reuses the Hinge message's architecture **in reverse register**: Gather's specific
gratitude does the systematic work (*thanks for the pavlova*), with room for Kate's line where she
wants one — "the journey's first message and its last are the same instrument." Kate's review pass
before send stands regardless.

Hinge spec §5: the outgoing ask has three movements, seam deliberately visible — (1) Kate speaks
first, fully hers; (2) the handover, Kate introduces Gather; (3) Gather takes over in its own
voice. The guest can tell whose words are whose, and that's the design.

Building this composition pattern once and using it for both the ask (I2, GTC-189) and the
thank-you (H1, GTC-186) avoids two divergent implementations of the same seam-visible idea.

### Scope

**Fix here:**
- A shared message-composition component/service implementing the three-movement pattern
  (author's-line / handover / system's-voice), with per-movement authorship
  (Kate-only / Gather-only / mixed) configurable per use.
- Support both directions: ask-register (Kate's ask, then Gather's roadmap) and thank-you-register
  (Gather's gratitude first, Kate's line optional) — per §8.4's "reverse register" framing.

**Do not touch:**
- All Do-Not-Touch Zones in `GATHER-BUILD-CONSTANTS.md`.
- The actual send/dispatch mechanism — that's I2's (the ask) and H1's (the thank-you) territory;
  this ticket builds message composition, not delivery.

### Founder ruling — multi-item ask construction (Nigel, 2026-08-23)

Resolves open decision 1 from the GTC-187 Phase 1 audit (multi-item ask construction). The
ticket's other open decisions from that audit are **not** resolved by this ruling.

- One message per person, regardless of item count.
- The message **names** the items asked for. It does not carry their logistics (quantity,
  drop-off location, timing).
- Logistics live on the guest tap page, not in the message.
- The tap page is **one screen**: all of the person's items listed, each with its own
  yes/no/maybe control.
- A single all-at-once control answers every item in one tap. It must state explicitly what it
  is agreeing to — a bare "Yes" is not acceptable when multiple items are listed.
- The all-at-once control is **hidden** when the person holds only one item.
- Per-item controls carry all three states, not just yes/no. A person may accept some items and
  decline or maybe others.

**Flag for downstream, not a decision:** a person who answers every item has nothing pending, so
their Moment 4 box is green — but a declined item becomes unassigned and is the host's to solve.
Where unassigned items surface in the grid is an open GTC-192 decision; this ruling makes that
concrete rather than theoretical.

### Founder ruling — persisting Kate's authored first movement (Nigel, 2026-08-23)

Resolves open decision 2 from the GTC-187 Phase 1 audit (persisting Kate's authored first
movement). No other open decision from that audit is resolved by this ruling.

- The host's authored line (movement 1) is **stored**, not ephemeral.
- On a mini-send, the stored line is presented to the host as an editable starting point —
  pre-filled, not sent silently.
- Editing at mini-send time is one gesture, not a required step. The common case (send as-is)
  costs nothing.

**Flag as a consequence, not a decision:**
- No schema field exists to hold this today. A field is needed, and schema changes fall under a
  Do-Not-Touch zone — so the field's design is its own decision, not settled here.

  out. The ledger implication differs between the two.~~ **RULED 2026-08-29 — see "Founder
  ruling — a mini-send edit OVERWRITES" below: overwrite. One value, last write wins.**

### Founder ruling — the drafted starting point for movement 1 (Nigel, 2026-08-23)

Resolves open decision 3 from the GTC-187 Phase 1 audit (the drafted starting point for movement
1). No other open decision from that audit is resolved by this ruling.

- The draft **is** generated from event data — name, date, venue, occasion, and whatever else the
  event already carries. Not a generic template, not a blank field.
- It is a starting point the host is expected to edit, not finished copy to approve. Presentation
  must make that clear.

**Flag as consequences, not decisions:**
- This puts Gather's words in the host's voice in movement 1, which is the movement the Hinge
  spec designates as fully hers. The seam the three-movement structure protects is thinnest here.
  Whatever is built should make editing the obvious next step rather than sending as-is.
- Open and **not** settled: whether the draft is pre-filled into the field or offered alongside an
  empty one.
- Open and **not** settled: whether generation is AI or templated from event fields. AI generation
  would be a new call on the send path; templating would not.

### Founder ruling — SMS/email content parity (Nigel, 2026-08-23)

Resolves open decision 5 from the GTC-187 Phase 1 audit (SMS and email content parity). No other
open decision from that audit is resolved by this ruling.

- One composed text serves both channels. Email adds a subject line; the body is identical.
- No per-channel shaping of the movements.
- Matches existing precedent in the nudge and wrap-up paths.

### Founder ruling — length budget (Nigel, 2026-08-23)

Resolves open decision 6 from the GTC-187 Phase 1 audit (length budget). No other open decision
from that audit is resolved by this ruling.

- No enforced cap. Multi-segment SMS is acceptable and already ships.
- Surface the segment count to the host at compose time so the cost is visible.
- Do not truncate, do not block.

### Founder ruling — per-movement authorship representation (Nigel, 2026-08-23)

Resolves open decision 7 from the GTC-187 Phase 1 audit (how per-movement authorship is
represented). No other open decision from that audit is resolved by this ruling.

**Shape: closed register presets, named content fields.**

```ts
type Voice = 'HOST' | 'SYSTEM';
interface VoiceProfile { authorLine: Voice; handover: Voice; systemVoice: Voice; }

const ASK_REGISTER: VoiceProfile = { authorLine: 'HOST', handover: 'HOST', systemVoice: 'SYSTEM' };

function composeMessage(
  content: { authorLine: string | null; handover: string; systemVoice: string },
  profile: VoiceProfile
): string
```

An invalid authorship combination cannot be constructed, because a register is a baked constant
rather than assembled per call. Field names match the spec's own vocabulary for the three
movements directly.

**`THANK_YOU_REGISTER` is deliberately not recorded.** Checked against Moment 4 §8.4, quoted in
full: *"The mix reuses the Hinge message's architecture in reverse register: Gather's specific
gratitude does the systematic work (thanks for the pavlova), with room for Kate's line where she
wants one — the journey's first message and its last are the same instrument."* This describes a
**role** inversion — Gather now supplies the specific content Kate supplied in the ask register,
and Kate's line becomes optional rather than mandatory-first — but it never states a per-movement
mapping: it does not say which named slot (`authorLine` / `handover` / `systemVoice`) Gather now
occupies, whether `handover` survives in this direction at all, or whether the movements keep
their order. "Reverse register" is glossed nowhere as either "invert the voice at each position"
or "reverse the sequence of movements" — both readings fit the quoted text equally, and nothing
else in §8.4 disambiguates. Recording a `THANK_YOU_REGISTER` here would invent the missing
mapping rather than read it off the spec. Left open for a founder ruling.

### Founder ruling — the itemless guest (Nigel, 2026-08-23)

Resolves open decision 8 from the GTC-187 Phase 1 audit (the itemless guest, the attendance-only
degenerate case). All eight open decisions from that audit are now recorded.

- An itemless guest receives all three movements. The handover to Gather is **not** dropped —
  the introduction is doing work for later: a guest who never met Gather would receive an
  unrecognised nudge if they go quiet.
- Movement 3 is thinner: no ask, no logistics. It carries what to expect and the roadmap only.
- The itemless guest taps through to the **same** guest page as everyone else, with zero item
  rows, and reaches the same confirmation screen. The page is one shape with a variable number of
  rows: three for a multi-item guest, one for a single-item guest, zero for an itemless one.
- Attendance is asked as a direct question **only** for guests with no items. For guests holding
  items, attendance is implied by their item responses, consistent with the existing
  `deriveAttendance` behaviour.

**Flag as a consequence, not a decision:** this means a guest who is attending but bringing
nothing answers a different question from a guest who is bringing something. Someone who
declines every item has answered items, not attendance — whether they are still coming is not
directly established. Whether that matters is not settled here.

### Executor finding — em dashes double the SMS cost (2026-08-29)

Not a decision and not a reopening of any ruling. Recorded here because it changes what
decision 6 ("surface the segment count so the cost is visible") actually shows a host, and
because it is not confined to this ticket.

**The mechanism.** GSM-7 has no em dash. A single `—` anywhere in a body forces the whole
message onto UCS-2, which drops the segment size from 160 characters to 70. The character
count does not change; the bill roughly doubles.

**Measured on `Henderson Family Christmas 2026` in the development database.** One em dash
was the ONLY non-ASCII character in every composed body:

| Recipient | items | characters | before | after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aarav Patel-Henderson | 1 | 510 | 8 segments | 4 |
| Rob Henderson | 4 | 489 | 7 segments | 4 |
| Amy Henderson (itemless) | 0 | 466 | 7 segments | 3 |

**Fixed here, for the ask register's own words only.** `authorLineFor`, `askHandover` and
`askSystemVoice` in `src/lib/messages/ask-register.ts` now use ` - `. `askSubject` in the
same file deliberately keeps its em dash: decision 5 puts the subject on email only, email
has no segments, and the cost argument does not reach it. `tests/message-composition-test.ts`
layer 1i pins the body inside GSM-7 so this cannot regress silently.

**What composition cannot control.** Item names, venue names, event names and person names
come from the database. One macron — `kūmara`, `Ōtautahi` — puts the same message back on
70-character segments, and no register can prevent that. The test asserts exactly this: the
register's half is clean, and a non-GSM-7 item name still flips the count.

**⚠ THE SAME DEFECT SHIPS IN THE NUDGE TEMPLATES, AND IS NOT FIXED HERE.** Every
system-sent template in `src/lib/sms/nudge-templates.ts` carries one em dash as its only
non-GSM-7 character, measured the same day with a production-shaped link:

| Symbol | characters | segments now | if GSM-7 clean |
|---|---|---|---|
| `getFirstNudgeMessage` | 207 | 3 | 2 |
| `getSecondNudgeMessage` | 211 | 4 | 2 |
| `getProxyHouseholdReminderMessage` | 219 | 4 | 2 |
| `getDecideByFollowupMessage` | 173 | 3 | 2 |
| `getHostNudgeMessage('direct', …)` | 142 | 3 | 1 |
| `getHostNudgeMessage('warm', …)` | 148 | 3 | 3 — carries an emoji too |

**Recommendation: its own ticket, and not folded into GTC-187 or GTC-189.** Four reasons.

1. **Different owner.** These templates are E1/E2's shipped machinery (GTC-178, GTC-179),
   both closed. GTC-187 builds composition and has no claim on them; editing them here would
   be an unscoped change to closed work.
2. **It touches the opt-out suffix.** The em dash in the auto-nudge templates sits inside
   ` — Reply STOP to opt out`, which `getDecideByFollowupMessage`'s own comment calls
   "mandatory on every system-sent template and is not a stylistic choice". Any edit to that
   string wants its own evidence and review, not a drive-by inside a composition ticket.
3. **It needs a ruling this ticket cannot make.** `getHostNudgeMessage`'s `warm` and `casual`
   variants carry emoji deliberately, and emoji are unfixable by punctuation — they are
   UCS-2 whatever the dashes do. Someone has to decide whether those variants stay expensive
   on purpose. That is a voice decision, not an executor's.
4. **The multiplier is larger there than here.** The ask sends once per person. The nudge
   sweep runs every fifteen minutes and can fire twice per unresponsive guest, on every
   event, forever.

Not filed by this ticket.

### Founder ruling — a mini-send edit OVERWRITES the stored line (Nigel, 2026-08-29)

Resolves the open question left by the decision-2 ruling above, and nothing else. The other
two open questions recorded in this ticket — decision 3's "pre-filled vs offered alongside an
empty field", and `THANK_YOU_REGISTER`'s missing per-movement mapping — remain open.

- The stored line holds **one value**. An edit at mini-send time **replaces** it. Last write wins.
- No per-send version is retained on the field. The field answers "what does Kate say", not
  "what did each guest receive".

**Rationale, recorded:** versioning costs a second storage shape — a row per send rather than a
value on the event — and the reuse case does not need one. The case that motivated storage at
all is the late-added guest getting *the same* line everyone else got; that is served by one
current value, not by history.

**⚠ Premise correction, and it does not change the ruling.** The founder's stated rationale
included "there is already a record of what was actually sent". **There is not, today.**
Verified 2026-08-29:

- `sendSms` logs `messageLength: message.length` into `InviteEvent.metadata` — the length, never
  the body (`src/lib/sms/send-sms.ts`, the `NUDGE_SENT_AUTO` success log).
- The ledger (`AuditEntry`, GTC-168) records scoped `before`/`after` on plan changes. It is not
  a message log, and §2's own rule — "the ledger stays meaningful because it is never asked to
  hold noise" — says it should not become one.
- The ask's send path does not exist yet at all; it is [[GTC-189]]'s.

What the ledger *would* give, if the write site routes through `logAudit()`, is a `before`/`after`
pair per **edit** — the line's own history, free, without a second storage shape. That is a
different record from "what each guest received", and it is the one this ruling's reuse case
actually wants. **Recording what was sent, per recipient, is [[GTC-189]]'s to decide** — this
ruling neither requires nor forecloses it.

**Consequence:** a nullable single-value column. Filed as [[GTC-259]]; the field is not added by
this ticket.
