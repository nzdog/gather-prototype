# GTC-318 — A child's attendance answer that was never asked

**Status:** Open — filed 2026-09-18, not started | **Branch:** TBD
**Severity:** [ ] Critical [ ] High [x] Medium [ ] Low — proposed at filing; the founder's to
set. `severity_proposed` carries the proposal and `severity` stays empty until it is set.
Escalated (brand/trust risk): [ ] Yes [x] No — it is wrong data on two fixture rows, not a
guest-facing failure.

⚠ **A PRECONDITION OF [[GTC-219]], AND THAT IS THE WHOLE URGENCY.** GTC-219 drops
`PersonEvent.rsvpStatus`, and `rsvpStatus` is the only evidence of where these two values came
from. Clean or rule on them **before** GTC-219, or the provenance goes with the column.

*Filed on founder instruction, 2026-09-18, at the [[GTC-191]] shape proposal: "Clean them, and
file it, because the deadline is real. File it against GTC-219 as a precondition."*

---

### Signal

**Expected**
A child's attendance is asked nowhere in the model. [[GTC-189]]'s founder ruling routes a
child's item through their household contact; Hinge §3's attendance question is asked of the
person answering about themselves. So `PersonEvent.attendanceAnswer` should be empty on every
CHILD row, and `deriveAttendance` should never answer YES or NO for a child on the strength of
a stored answer.

**Actual**
Two CHILD memberships carry `attendanceAnswer: 'YES'`. `deriveAttendance` in
`src/lib/attendance.ts` returns YES for both, because rule 2 — an explicit answer — outranks
everything below it. Any reader asking "is this child attending?" gets an answer the model
never posed.

**Smallest failing case**
`SELECT` any `PersonEvent` with `householdRole = 'CHILD'` and `attendanceAnswer IS NOT NULL`.

---

### Reproduce

**Required state** — none. It is data already in `gather_dev`.

**Observe — measured 2026-09-18, read-only**

- 12 `PersonEvent` rows carry an `attendanceAnswer`.
- **9 of them carry an answer identical to their legacy `rsvpStatus`, with
  `attendanceAnsweredAt` exactly equal to `rsvpRespondedAt` — both null.**
- Exactly 9 rows still carry a legacy `YES` or `NO` in `rsvpStatus`. One to one.
- All 9 are on one fixture event, **"GTC-129 Test BBQ"**: seven adults and **two children**
  (Oliver Smith, Aanya Patel).
- Neither child has an `attendanceAnsweredAt`, an `AuditEntry` with the child as `actorId`, an
  `AccessToken`, or an `rsvpRespondedAt`. **Nothing asked and nobody answered.**
- The other 3 rows with an `attendanceAnswer` are Ray Dalton on the [[GTC-192]] boards, written
  directly by `scripts/seed-gtc192-replay.ts` and `scripts/seed-gtc192-phase7.ts` with a real
  timestamp. They do not match the fingerprint and are not this ticket's.

---

### Where the values came from — the fingerprint, asserted; the moment, not

The nine rows carry the exact signature the [[GTC-174]] backfill migration describes in its own
comment (`prisma/migrations/20260805051356_gtc174_backfill_attendance_answer/migration.sql`):

> `attendanceAnsweredAt` inherits `rsvpRespondedAt`, which may itself be NULL on a row whose
> status was set without a timestamp. Such a row keeps its answer and loses only the time of it.

And no path in `src` writes `rsvpStatus` any more, while the only writer of `attendanceAnswer`
— `PATCH` in `src/app/api/p/[token]/route.ts` — always stamps `attendanceAnsweredAt` in the same
transaction. So a row carrying an answer with no timestamp did not come through the live path.

⚠ **THE CAVEAT, RECORDED RATHER THAN BURIED.** [[GTC-174]]'s evidence records that backfill as a
**verified no-op at apply time** — 61 `PersonEvent` rows, all `PENDING`. There are 335 rows now.
So either these nine rows postdate that apply, or this database met the migration at a different
moment than the one GTC-174 recorded. **That cannot be distinguished from inside the database.
The fingerprint is what this ticket asserts; the moment is not.**

**The backfill did not single children out, and could not have.** It keys on `rsvpStatus` and
has no idea what a `householdRole` is. The two children were carried across for the same reason
the seven adults were. That is the point: the old model asked one event-level RSVP question of
everybody, and the ruling that abolished it did not reach back into who had been asked.

### Scope

**Fix here:**
- The two CHILD rows' `attendanceAnswer` / `attendanceAnsweredAt` in `gather_dev`, and whatever
  equivalent exists in production — see Unknown 2.
- If the answer is a guard rather than only a clean: the write path is `PATCH` in
  `src/app/api/p/[token]/route.ts`, which already refuses 409 unless `isAttendanceAskable`. A
  child-row refusal would sit beside it.

**Do not touch:**
- All Do-Not-Touch Zones in `GATHER-BUILD-CONSTANTS.md` are binding.
- **The seven adult rows.** Their answers are real answers guests gave under the old model and
  [[GTC-174]] carried them deliberately: *"dropping them would silently un-answer people who had
  answered."* Only the CHILD rows are wrong.
- **`deriveAttendance`'s precedence.** Rule 2 outranking rules 3–5 is correct and documented. The
  defect is the stored value, not the derivation reading it.
- The three Ray Dalton rows on the [[GTC-192]] boards.

### Unknowns

1. **Clean only, or clean and guard?** A data fix leaves the write path able to do it again —
   today only because no child is ever sent a link, which [[GTC-316]] shows is thinner than it
   sounds. [[GTC-191]]'s carried write is scoped to `Assignment.response` and cannot reach
   `attendanceAnswer`, so the guard may be redundant by construction. That is a judgement about
   how much construction-deep is enough, and it is the founder's.
2. **Production.** Every figure here is `gather_dev`. Whether production carries rows with the
   same fingerprint is unmeasured and must be measured before anything is written there.
3. **A migration, or a script?** A data fix with no schema diff is [[GTC-174]]'s own
   `--create-only` pattern. A one-off script leaves no record. Recorded as a choice, not made.
