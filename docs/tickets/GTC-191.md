## Founder rulings — 2026-09-18, at the shape proposal and after it

**Word 1 — THE PAGE CARRIES THE ATTRIBUTION, NOT THE LINE.**

> FROM KATE. Your first option.

Recorded with the founder's reasons: the line is absent by default on all 29 events in
`gather_dev`, so "with" would render a generated sentence as a personal one, which is worse than
showing nothing; the `''` state means it can be deliberately empty, so it can never be
structural; and Hinge §5's movements are the MESSAGE's structure, not the page's — movement 2 is
a handover that has already happened by the time she is here. And it is a swap rather than an
addition: *"Participant: Amelia Turner"* was the host's dashboard language pointed at a guest,
which §3 would cut anyway. The words already exist in `askSubject`, so no new vocabulary.

**Word 2 — A CHILD'S NO RAISES NOTHING.**

> NO. A child's no raises nothing.

§3's follow-up exists because a no to the item is ambiguous between can't-bring-that and
can't-come, and that ambiguity is a property of someone answering about themselves. The model
never asks whether a child is coming, so there is no ambiguity to resolve.

⚠ **AND THE RULING IS HELD BY THE SEPARATION, NOT BY A SCREEN CONVENTION.** `isAttendanceAskable`
is TRUE the moment the carrier declines the child's only row, because all-declined is one of its
two true cases. The follow-up would appear by itself, unruled, if the child's array drove a beat.
It drives no beat and no derivation, and the carried write reaches `Assignment.response` and
nothing else, so a child's `PersonEvent.attendanceAnswer` is unwritable by construction.

**Zone 3 — SIGNED, for five things and nothing else.**

> The three route changes, the separated GET field, the audit line naming both people, the 403,
> and the security fence. No issuance, no scope, no schema. If the build needs any of those,
> HALT.

**And the chooser re-run is what makes it safe.**

> One predicate, two callers, no second reading of A2 to drift from. A household lookup would
> reintroduce exactly the defect GTC-294's note caught.

**The host stays on [[GTC-297]]**, and slice 5 may press without it. The carried section is
driven by the chooser's `CARRIED` route rather than by the token's scope, so GTC-297's link has
one job when it comes: resolve to the same (event, person) context.

**The `LinkState` work is [[GTC-189]] slice 5's, and it goes FIRST.**

> Decision 29 says the pre-flight must show it before the press, and a state written after the
> gap closes is a state nobody has seen fire.

### What was built

**`src/lib/eligibility/carried-answer.ts`** — new. `resolveCarriedSubjects` and `mayAnswerFor`.
The authorisation is `chooseAskRoute` re-run at the moment of the read or the write: the write is
admitted only where the chooser answers `CARRIED` with the token holder's membership as the
recipient. Five refusals therefore hold by construction rather than by inspection — an adult is
never carried, a child of a household that did not pick her never resolves, a child of the host's
own household is refused `HOST_HOUSEHOLD_CHILD`, a muted household is refused `HOUSEHOLD_MUTED`,
and a child holding nothing is `CHILD_WITHOUT_ITEM`.

**`GET /api/p/[token]`** — returns `carried`, a separate array, one entry per child with that
child's own rows. Her `assignments` field is unchanged and still drives `attendance`,
`attendanceAnswer` and `attendanceAskable` alone. Both arrays map through one `wireAssignment`,
so the child's item cannot quietly carry less detail than hers — the message says "the details
are on the page" about the child's item too. `event.hostFirstName` added for word 1.

**`POST /api/p/[token]/ack/[assignmentId]`** — takes a carried answer. The ownership check
becomes "hers, or a child's whose ask her message carries", computed before the transaction and
re-checked inside it against the row as it then stands, so a reassignment between the two fails
closed. A row that exists and is not answerable gets **403**; a row that is nobody's still gets
404. The audit line names both people: `actorId` is the carrier, `targetId` is the child's
`Assignment`, and `details` carries "on behalf of {name}".

**`src/app/p/[token]/page.tsx`** — two sections, never interleaved, hers first, one section per
child. One shared `AssignmentCard` renders both, with `onBehalfOf` changing the words and nothing
else. `Participant: {name}` replaced by `from {hostFirstName}`.

**`tests/security-validation.ts`** — two assertions: a PARTICIPANT token cannot answer another
participant's assignment, with a control that the same token still answers its own.

## ⚠ THE FINDING — an assertion that measured something adjacent to what it claimed

**It is the sixth of its family in this ledger, and the first caught without a disagreement.**

The founder asked for one case checked against the tree rather than against a fixture, and named
the subject: Amelia Turner on the "GTC-192 glance — mixed" board, whose own two rows are MAYBE
and whose carried child holds an ACCEPTED row. Layer A(tree) of
`tests/carried-answer-test.ts` was written to hold it, in two assertions:

```
SEPARATE: deriveAttendance(herRows, herAnswer)            is not YES
MERGED:   deriveAttendance([...herRows, ...kidRows], …)   is YES
```

**Both of them call `deriveAttendance` on arrays the test builds, and `deriveAttendance` was
never changed by this ticket.** So the pair asserts that the data still has the shape the case
needs and that the helper does arithmetic. **It says nothing about whether the route keeps the
two arrays apart** — the thing the layer's own header claims it holds. It is green on the fixed
tree and green on the unfixed one, and the RED run proves it: all four Layer A(tree) assertions
passed at RED, in a run where 18 others failed.

**The defect is in the instruction as much as in the test.** The founder's words, 2026-09-18:

> My prompt asked for a case checked against the tree and I did not think about what it would be
> measuring; the error is mine as much as the test's.

**The family.** [[GTC-273]], where the route scanner's FAIL-OPEN detector matched the spelling of
a credential rather than evaluating it; [[GTC-276]], where a detector inherited the defect the
ticket that wrote it had just named; ruling AN's two refusals sharing a why-code; ruling AH's
measurement reading an answer's kind and never its channel; and the 231-to-1 channel split that
was exactly right and attributed to the wrong cause. All of them are checks that examine
something adjacent to the thing they claim to examine, and all of them pass convincingly while
doing it.

⚠ **AND WHAT MAKES THIS ONE DIFFERENT IS THE WAY IT WAS CAUGHT.** Every earlier instance was
caught by a SECOND measurement disagreeing with the first — a raw channel shape that would not
reconcile, a control that fired where it should not have. **This one had nothing to disagree with
it.** It was caught by reading the assertion against its own claim: the layer said it held the
separation, the assertion computed a pure function of two arrays, and those two sentences do not
meet. A check with no second opinion is not therefore trustworthy; it is only unexamined.

**The rule this leaves.** *An assertion is a claim about the code only where the code is between
the input and the output. Where a test builds the input and calls a helper the change did not
touch, it is a claim about the helper — however exactly its subject was chosen, and however
carefully the case was taken from the tree.*

**Left as it stands**, on the founder's instruction to commit as built. The repair is to assert
against the route's own payload — `carried` separate from `assignments`, and `attendance` read
off the response rather than recomputed — which is where layers A(fixture) and B already measure
Bea and Cara, and is why those two failed at RED and this one did not.

## The four assertions that pass at RED, kept deliberately

Beside the finding above sits its opposite, and the distinction is the point. Several assertions
in this suite were green before the change and are kept:

- the page never concatenates the child's rows into her own;
- `GET` derives attendance from the holder's own assignments only;
- the ack route reaches no `Household` directly;
- a child's `PersonEvent.attendanceAnswer` is untouched by a carried write, and so is hers.

**An assertion that cannot fail today is worth keeping when the thing it pins is a RULE rather
than a behaviour.** None of these describes what the change made the code do; each describes a
shape the code must keep — and each names the exact edit a future reader would otherwise make in
good faith. That is different in kind from Layer A(tree), which claimed to hold a behaviour and
measured a helper instead.
